# Champion recipe: Geography, maps & geopolitical mysteries

The writer follows this over the shared prompts' defaults (which often use money stories, a 28–35 s
length and money figures as examples). The hard rules do not change: true, sourced facts; figures from code or from a
sourced `facts` entry; one narrator; captions of every word.

## The story

- **One place, one question.** Every Short is about a real place (a country, border, river, sea, city,
  route) and asks one question about it: *why is it like that?* or *what if it changed?*
- **Topic kinds:** what if: a world that changed (1 in 2); why a place is shaped or placed like that
  (1 in 2); a ranking now and then as a test. Resources and the economy only as a place story.
- **True:** every figure and claim is a sourced fact (area, distance, population, date, count). A
  "what if" is clearly a thought experiment, built from real figures ("8 billion citizens", "about
  180 currencies").
- **Disputed borders** follow the official map of the country the video is about, or are left out.

## The first second

- The title's question is the first spoken line, over the map it's about, the place named in it.
- No figure in the first 3 seconds; the first figure arrives by about 10 s, as a comparison the viewer
  can feel ("1,000 km longer than India from Kashmir to Kanyakumari", "you could fit Belgium in it 40
  times").
- A claim whose answer waits for the end is allowed ("Only one country here rolls like a real wheel").
- Talk to the viewer once ("your country is in there somewhere").

## Length and pace

- **60–80 seconds**, about 185 words a minute, 180–240 words.
- A chain of causes: each cause is a new place or a change on the map, every 3 seconds or so.
- The payoff is the most surprising fact, near the end; the last line loops into the first frame.
- A countdown or a race (DAY 1…7, "14 of 170 still rolling") is welcome when the story has steps.

## The look (directed 2D)

- **The map is the stage** (owner, 2026-11-17, on the first sample: "this niche is all about animation
  with maps and show story in that"). At least 9 of every 10 beats are drawn on the map (the references:
  maps in 35 of 59 shots, characters on screen a median 0% of the time). A story's places are spots on the
  map (the river mouth, a border crossing, the capital), never rooms or landscapes beside it.
- **The story happens on the map**: flags planted on the land, a fort or a ship standing on its real spot,
  borders that draw themselves, countries that fill, merge and crack, boats moving along the river, a
  treaty's ruler and pen drawing a line onto the map. The camera moves over the map itself (a push-in is
  a bigger map scale, a pull-back shows the whole region).
- **Characters are small, standing on the map** where the story puts them (a diplomat on the north bank,
  a girl planting a flag at Banjul), never a full-frame scene; historical figures as photo heads on our
  bodies, never speaking.
- Figures on things in the place: a sign, a stall's price board, a counter, a label on the map.
- The camera pushes in on the place, pulls back to show scale, follows a route; a country can be lifted
  and laid over another for scale.
- Captions: the channel's directed captions; place names as labels on the map.

## Words

- Plain words; a place name the first time it's used comes with where it is ("Gambia, a thin strip in
  West Africa").
- No "Did you know" as a first line, no "Watch this"; the question is the hook.

## Every video its own story device and animation (owner, 2026-11-18)

"Don't ever make every topic animation / story style the same." Each video picks one **main device**
the story is told through, different from the last three videos, and names it in the plan (`device`):
- **paint and wrap**: a country made on screen, its neighbour drawn round it (the Gambia sample)
- **route and race**: ships, planes or trucks on routes, a detour drawn as it costs time (a canal, a strait)
- **countdown**: DAY 1, 2, 3… or a month counter, one consequence a step, the map changing each step
- **lift and compare**: a shape lifted and laid over a place the viewer knows, at true scale
- **timeline**: a year slider, borders redrawn and countries recoloured year by year
- **zoom from space**: the globe, then a dive to the place, then back out for the payoff
- **erase and redraw**: borders rubbed out and drawn again a new way (a what-if)
- **flood or grow**: water, desert or people spreading across the map as the figure climbs
The look stays the channel's (the vintage map, its sound); the device, the camera's path, the colours
in play and the places change. Two videos in a row never open on the same picture.

## Keep it moving (owner, 2026-11-18)

- Every word a line lists is shown on its word ("phones, clothes, cars and oil": a phone box, a clothes
  bale, a car carrier, a tanker, one after another). Write lists that can be pictured.
- Something new on screen at least every 1.8 s (`channel.json` `motion.maxGapSec`), and small life in
  between: ships sailing, labels writing on, pins pulsing, the camera drifting over the map.
