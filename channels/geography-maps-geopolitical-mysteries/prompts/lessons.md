# Lessons from our own videos

Each lesson is a mistake that cost viewers, written by the reviewer (agents/reviewer.md) from where viewers
left. The writer follows them and QA enforces them. A new channel starts with channel #1's general ones.

## 1. The second line delivers a thing you can see, not a setup (new, 24 Nov 2026)
Seen in: every Short with drop points loses its steepest share at the second beat (5.4-6.0 s), and the three
steepest falls there were lines that made the viewer wait or work:
- v-20261120-04 (Dinorwig) 32.4% at 5.8 s: "Under the grass of Elidir Fawr, in Snowdonia, there is a cave 51 m
  high..." (two names nobody knows, and the picture leaves the map for a cave).
- v-20261122-11 (English cities, stayed 48.9%, 10 points under the channel's 59%) 19.1% at 5.4 s: "From London
  to the mill towns of the north, it happens again and again. And the reason isn't on the ground. It's in the
  air." (a tease with no fact; on screen a plain Britain with three pins, then at 10 s an empty Britain).
- v-20261121-03 (Tristan da Cunha, stayed 47.9%, 11 points under) 17.7% at 6.0 s and 5.9% more at 11.7 s, same
  beat: "Its nearest neighbour, Saint Helena, sits 2,430 km to the north, and South Africa is 2,816 km away to
  the east." (two figures in one 8-second sentence; at 5 s the frame is empty ocean, a dashed line and a dot,
  and Saint Helena and South Africa only appear by 10 s).
- Repeated after this lesson was written (27 Nov): v-20261125-11 (Australia's dingo fence, made 24 Nov 23:18
  UTC; stayed 48.8%, the channel's lowest, 9.5 points under the 58.3% median) 13.9% at 5.7 s: "It starts on the
  cliffs of the south coast: wire 180 centimetres high, taller than you." The beat opens at 4.3 s on bare sand
  and a dashed line; the cliffs only rise at 5.5 s and the wire and height marker at 6.7-7.8 s, after the drop.
- v-20261124-12 (Panama, made before this lesson; stayed 49.4%, 8.9 points under): the second line is a pure
  question, "So what if it never rained there again?", over an unlabelled blue lake; the first figure waits for
  the third beat (9.1% more left on "36 to just 22" at 9.9 s).
The mildest second-beat falls were one thing shown on its word: Lesotho's hole (v-20261124-11, "That hole is a
country, Lesotho.": no top drop at the second beat; its steepest, 14.9% at 8.1 s, came on the next line, which
stacks 30,355 km², Belgium and "every road out of it"), Denmark laid over Greenland (v-20261119-02, 15.3%), the
193 flags coming down (v-20261120-05, 15.1%).
Fix: the second line is one short sentence (about 15 words, under 5 s) with one fact or one figure, about a
place already on screen, and the thing it names appears on the map on its word. No "the reason is..." tease,
no second unfamiliar place name, no two figures in it, and no stacking a figure, a comparison place and a
second fact in the third line either. Director: the second beat opens with its thing already in frame (the
wire, the cliff, the lane), drawn in on the beat's first word, never an empty sea, bare land or a dashed line
waiting for its moment a second later.

## 2. The first frame names the subject (general; raised 24 Nov 2026)
Seen in: channel #1's first month, 55-76% swiped away when the title and first second held the subject back.
Here: v-20261122-11 "Why are English cities poorer in the east?" opens on a made-up diagram labelled "OLD
ENGLISH CITY" (no real city is ever named or shown until 5 s), and stayed to watch was 48.9%, 10 points under
the channel's median of 59%; the Shorts whose first frame was a real named place filling the screen
(Greenland 60.8%, Hormuz 60.7%) held best.
Fix: the title and the first spoken sentence name who or what the video is about, and the first frame shows
that real, named place on the map (a city by its name, not "an old city").

## 3. The map must answer the question, not just sit under it (owner-marked wrong, 20 Nov 2026)
Seen in: v-20261120-04 "Why did Britain hide a power station inside a mountain?" (20 Nov). Every beat was
labelled "on the map", but the question was engineering (how a pumped-storage plant works), so the map was a
close-up of one hill and the answer came from a cut-away of the rock (10–20 s), a battery cartoon (25 s) and a
stopwatch (50 s). The owner marked it wrong: "Anything topics that are explained not in the map are wrong…
Make sure this does not happen again." 43 views on its first day, against 1,207 (Greenland) and 685 (Sahara),
whose answers are a border, a position or a shape you can see. Retention (24 Nov): it then lost 32.4% at 5.8 s,
the moment the picture left the map for a cave under Elidir Fawr, the steepest drop of any of our Shorts.
Fix: before writing, say the answer in one line as something on the map (a border, a distance, a shape, a
route, where a place sits next to another). If the answer is a machine, a law or a habit rather than a
where, don't write the script: pick another topic. Zoom no closer than a region, and never cut away from
the map to explain how something works.

## 4. Only what is true (general)
Seen in: channel #1, invented examples lost to real, named stories.
Fix: a true channel tells sourced facts about real, named people or things; a fiction channel says so.

## 5. No reaction shots (general)
Seen in: channel #1, shots of a character reacting were where viewers left.
Fix: every picture shows the thing being said.

## 6. The payoff is the most surprising fact (general)
Fix: hold the most surprising figure or fact for the payoff, and make the last line loop into the first.

## 7. The map is the stage, not a backdrop between scenes
Seen in: our first sample (Gambia, 17 Nov 2026): 7 of 11 beats were cartoon scenes (a beach, a fort, a
treaty room, a street) and the map showed up only between them; the owner stopped it before render.
Fix: every beat but at most one is drawn on the map; the story's events and its small characters stand on
their real spots on it, and the camera moves over the map.

## 8. Narration is never slow (general)
Seen in: the owner's notes on How Tech Works (Shorts), Disaster Desk (long) and Rise & Fall: "increase the pace".
Fix: no channel's narrator speaks under about 164 words a minute in a Short or 189 in a long video (generator/pack.py MIN_SPEED enforces it).

## 9. Show what happens, not only the count (general)
Seen in: Disaster Desk's Hyatt long video, 22 Nov: "only showing death counts is not enough. Make that scenes."
Fix: when a line says what happened to people or things, the beat shows it happening in the place (the crowd under the walkway, the cars on the bridge), never gory (director.md).

## 10. Every picture once (general)
Seen in: Rise & Fall, 20 Nov: "the same 3 sneaker photos reused and almost no effects" (two Shorts marked stale).
Fix: a photo or picture is in one beat of a Short (the loop beat may repeat the first), two of a long video at most; the code check fails a repeat (renderer/remotion.py picture_once_problems).

## 11. A named place, company or person shows its real mark (general)
Seen in: Geography's Russia Short, 23 Nov: "Where is the flag of Russia??" (its area grew in plain red); earlier notes on the first finance channel's Shorts.
Fix: a country on the map shows its flag (the code check fails a map video whose named country never gets one), a company its logo, a person their photo head.

## 12. A long video's first sentence states the stakes (general)
Seen in: Disaster Desk's Hyatt long video, 22 Nov: the hook was "good only for people who want to see the full video".
Fix: the first sentence says the small cause and how big it got, in plain words (agents/studio.md); the title stays free of it.
