## What top Shorts do that we don't (titles, covers, first frame)
- **The title barely decides who finds us. The first frame does.**
  - About 96% of our views come from the Shorts feed, which shows the first frame, not the cover.
  - Our first frames already follow the rules: the named place fills the frame, its flag is on it, a source line sits at the top and the first caption word is showing. Nothing to change there tonight.
- **Titles: no new test.** Here's how the top 30 breakout titles break down, with the median views on each side:

  | Trait | Breakouts with it | Median with | Median without |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | An emoji | 17 | 217K | 174K |
  | A capitalised word ("SPLIT", "PEOPLE") | 6 | 275K | 168K |
  | Hashtags | 20 | 168K | 215K |

  - Those gaps are small, the groups are small, and they overlap with channels that make countryball videos, so they don't justify a test.
  - The median title is about 43 characters, and vidIQ says the feed cuts titles at about 40. Ours run 28–55, and the 55-character one (the Britain power station) has our best avg viewed.
  - What every one of the 30 does is name the place or subject. Ours already do. The running `geo-title-names-the-oddity` challenger covers the rest.
- **Covers often hide the place.** 5 of our last 8 cover lines are a pronoun:
  - "Why is it Danish?", "Who lives here?", "How did it grow?", "What is it stopping?", "Why the hole?"
  - The script's `thumbnail.tag` already holds the name ("Australia", "Lesotho"). `Thumbnail.tsx` only draws it inside the figure card, so a scene cover with no figure shows no name at all. The Dingo Fence and Africa covers have none.
  - Covers are seen only in search and on the channel page (about 4% of views), so this is a small, cheap experiment. Proposal `geo-cover-names-the-place`.
- **Voice** (nightly): no new evidence on top of `geo-pace-185`. Every what-if breakout I could see has one narrator. No change.

## Learn from our own numbers
- **Best by avg viewed:**
  - Britain's power station (82.5%): a why title naming the odd thing.
  - Greenland (74.4%).
  - Russia and the Urals (67.6%).
- **Worst:**
  - English cities (54.4%).
  - Suez (55%).
  - Sahara (56.6%).
- The pattern is the same as last night: titles that name a visible oddity beat titles that state a scenario. That's already the `geo-title-names-the-oddity` challenger, so there's nothing new to propose.
- The Britain power station Short got only 165 views despite the best hold. Its cover had zero click-through on 78 impressions, which fits the cover finding above.
