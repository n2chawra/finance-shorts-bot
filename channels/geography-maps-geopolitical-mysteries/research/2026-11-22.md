## Teardown: Odd Atlas and WorldShift
These are the two channels closest to us in format (60–70 s, map-led, one question) that beat their audience by the most.

**Odd Atlas** (5.25K subscribers; 53 Shorts in 60 days; median 56.7K views; nearly every Short 64–75 s).
- **Titles name the strange shape you can see.** 9 of its top 15 do:

  | Shape word | Views |

  | perfectly straight lines | 128K |
  | shaped like a river | 138K |
  | drawn with a ruler | 114K |
  | slice of Antarctica | 103K |

- **Its titles are plain sentences.** No emojis and no hashtags.
- **One question per Short**, always with a where for the answer.
- **Not for us:** a third of its biggest are disputed status (Gaza, Taiwan, the West Bank, Kosovo, Kashmir), which our profile rules out.

**WorldShift** (3.47K subscribers; 54 Shorts; median 57.8K; 45–70 s).
- **The whole world as the subject.** Its hits are what-ifs that change the whole world at once: one country (2.95M), the same population (160K), everyone refusing their debt (274K), the same land area (57K), swapping land (124K).
- **Not for us:** its other half is war what-ifs, which we skip.

**What they do that we don't:**
1. **The title names the oddity on the map**, not the place plus a fact. That goes on the weekly-loop list as challenger `geo-title-names-the-oddity` (first-second, judged on stayed to watch), placed after the claim-hook test. No numbers of ours exist yet to rank it higher.
2. **Whole-world what-ifs with a redrawn map.** One of tonight's topics tests that in the feed, not in the recipe.

**Already the same as us:** length (their 64–70 s against our 60–64 s) and one narrator with no on-camera face.

**Voice:** both use a single calm male narrator at an explainer pace, close to our bm_george at about 185 wpm. Nothing to change.
