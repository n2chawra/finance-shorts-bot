# Niche report: Geography, maps & geopolitical mysteries (17 Nov 2026)

## Method

- Scan (`scan.json`): 3 reference channels + 42 found by 6 searches in viewers' words; their last 6
  months of uploads; every video scored against its own channel's median. 1,397 quota units.
- Labels: 8 off-niche channels dropped whole (games, comedy, animal facts, gangster stories, exam
  lectures), sports and celebrity titles dropped; 1,941 titles labelled into 9 families by rules written
  from reading every channel's titles; ranked by `niche-research` (`families.json`).
- Watching: 5 top Shorts in the owner's Chrome, frames every 3–5 s, words from YouTube's transcript
  panel (`sheets/`): Why Chile is so long (10.6M), What if the world became one country (2.95M), Can
  countries roll down a hill (2.7M), How America bought Louisiana (18.7M), The secret route Russia uses
  to arm Iran (2.1M). Planned 8; 5 gave consistent targets and the rest would have cost usage for little.
- Not done: comments (403 from YouTube for our sign-in; an API key is needed), the sound study
  (videos were watched muted).

## Style (`style.json`)

Median 65 s; ~185 words a minute; maps in 35 of 59 shots; most shots move; text on screen half the time;
subject named at 0 s in 3 of 5; no figure in the first 3 s; 2 of 5 loop. Looks: flat political maps
with small characters (WorldShift, GeoGlobeTales), satellite terrain with a coloured country (Chile, Map
& Myth), 3D physics (the hill race; not our kit).

## Risks turned into setup rules

- Disputed borders follow the official map of the country a video is about, or are left out.
- Every fact sourced, as on Paisa Panchayat; a claim from reports ("according to multiple reports") is
  attributed on screen.
- No footage, no faces on camera; maps from Natural Earth (public domain).
- Today's wars only when the story is really about a place.
