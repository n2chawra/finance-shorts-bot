## The map test (owner's note, applied tonight)
Before a topic goes in the feed, its answer must be said in one line as something on the map: a border, a route, a shape, a distance, where a place sits. A machine, a law, a habit or a naming story fails (the reviewer's lesson #1 says the same for the writer). Every new topic now carries `map_answer`.

## Policy and algorithm (Saturday)
- **AI labels (May 2026).** YouTube's one disclosure label is for photorealistic or meaningfully altered AI content, and YouTube now adds it automatically when a creator doesn't. A label changes neither reach nor monetisation. Our videos are flat 2D cartoons on maps with a synthetic voice, not photorealistic, so nothing to do; the improver should keep the "altered or synthetic content" box honest at upload if YouTube ever asks about the voice.
- **Inauthentic content (mid-2026).** The monetisation crackdown targets repetitive, templated, mass-produced AI videos with no human creative input. The channel's protection is what the variety rules already enforce: a different place, story and picture every video. A second reason the owner's map test matters: the same story told over a decorative map, again and again, is what that policy describes.
- **Monetisation threshold.** One report says that from 1 Feb 2027, new Partner Program applicants need 20M Shorts views in 90 days, double today's 10M. It's a single source and doesn't affect what we make now; I'll re-check it before February.
- **"Engaged views" is a counting change, not a ranking change** (reported by TechCrunch and others). Our weekly loop already judges on engaged views ÷ views. Search Engine Journal reports analysts seeing Shorts older than 30 days get fewer views (YouTube hasn't confirmed). That favours fresh daily uploads, which we already do. **Nothing to change.**
