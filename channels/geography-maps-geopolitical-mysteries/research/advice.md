# Advice: Geography, maps & geopolitical mysteries

From the scan of 45 channels (3,600 videos, 17 Nov 2026), Claude's topic labels ranked by the numbers
(`families.json`), and 5 of the niche's top Shorts watched frame by frame (`sheets/`, `style.json`).

## Topic kinds to make

1. **What if: a world that changed** — core, 1.79× its channel's median, 20% of them hits. 1 in 2
   Shorts. Examples of ours: "What if India had no Himalayas?", "What if the Suez Canal closed for a
   year?", "What if every country swapped capitals?". Rests on: "What if the entire world became one
   country" (2.95M, WorldShift), "Can countries roll down a hill" (2.7M, 3.2K-subscriber channel).
2. **Why a place is shaped or placed like that** — core, 1.47×, 20% hits. 1 in 2 Shorts. Examples:
   "Why is Chile so long?" is taken; ours: "Why does Gambia sit inside Senegal?", "Why is Bangladesh
   almost surrounded by India?", "Why does Russia have a piece of land inside Europe?". Rests on:
   "Why Chile is so long & narrow" (10.6M, 14K channel), "Which country is almost a perfect circle?".
3. Now and then, **rankings and comparisons** (1.27×, low tier): a test, not a staple.

## Kinds to avoid, or test before relying on them

- **Rich, poor and resources: 0.33×** (43 Shorts). This is part of the concept you gave ("resources and
  the world economy"), so it isn't dropped: told as a *place* story ("Why is a desert country rich?")
  rather than an economics lesson, and tried as a challenger in the weekly loop first. Note: the
  niche's biggest Short, "How America bought Louisiana" (18.7M), was counted here only because its title
  has a "$"; it is really a how-a-place-came-to-be story, which is family 2.
- **Today's geopolitics (0.98×) and India's maps (0.89×)**: no better than average, and the riskiest
  (wars, disputed borders). Used only when the story is really about a place's shape or a what-if.

## How many to post

- **The research advises 1 Short a day**: the niche's channels with a hit post about 0.6 a day.
  At 1 a day each weekly-loop test takes about 16 days instead of 7; **2 a day** halves that and still
  fits the quota (5,014 units a day are free after Paisa Panchayat; each upload ~1,650).
- **No long videos at first**: only 1 small channel had a long hit (hit rate 3%). Long videos once one
  of ours passes 100K views.

## Shorts, long or both

Shorts only.

## The look

The hits show **maps first** (35 of 59 shots), then objects and small characters. Two looks win:

- **Flat maps with small characters** (WorldShift, GeoGlobeTales: a cartoon person on a flat political
  map, a DAY counter, figures on signs, historical figures as big-headed characters, one real portrait).
  This is our directed 2D style with photo heads: **recommended**.
- Satellite terrain maps with a country drawn in red over them (Chile, Map & Myth). Possible with
  Natural Earth's public-domain shaded relief; a second look to test later.

Needed before samples: **a map component** (country outlines from Natural Earth, public domain;
recolour, outline, drag a country onto another for scale, draw a route, pin a label). Nothing in the
niche needs footage or a face on camera; the one footage shot we saw (a ship) we would draw.

## The first second

- Name the place in the first sentence (3 of 5 hits did it at 0 s) and ask the question there: the
  title's question is the first line ("What if the entire world became one country?").
- No figure in the first 3 s in any of them: the hits open on the place and the question; the first
  figure comes around 7–14 s, often as a comparison the viewer knows ("1,000 km longer than India
  from Kashmir to Kanyakumari").
- A claim whose answer waits for the end works ("Only one country here rolls like a real wheel").
- Talk to the viewer's own country ("Your country is in there somewhere").

## Style targets (measured, 5 sheets)

- Length **60–80 s** (median 65 s): longer than Paisa Panchayat's Shorts; the niche's hits explain a
  chain of causes.
- **~185 words a minute**; the picture changes about every **3 s** (my sampling every 3–5 s reads 5.9 s,
  an overstatement); 82% of shots move (push in, pull out, follow a route).
- Text on screen about half the time (place names, labels); captions word by word or by phrase.
- 2 of 5 loop to their first frame; a DAY 1…7 or a race structure gives a countdown to finish.

## What the quick verdict got wrong or missed

- **The biggest small-channel hits are Indian.** "Why Chile is so long" (10.6M) is narrated in Hindi
  under an English title, and many of the small channels with hits are Indian (India's maps, Hindi
  "what if"). An English channel competes with WorldShift and GeoGlobeTales; a Hindi one would be a
  different channel (Kokoro has Hindi voices, untested by us). The verdict didn't see this.
- Comments couldn't be read (YouTube refused our sign-in for comments), so the topic ideas come from
  the titles and the hits, not from viewers' requests.
