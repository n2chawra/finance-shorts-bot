# How Tech Works

**What it is.** A YouTube channel that shows how the technology people use every day actually works, all
the way down: chips, AI, phones, the internet, data centres and the machines that power them. Every video
is a 3D animated explainer: a lit object on a dark stage, opened up, taken apart and zoomed into until the
mechanism is visible. Long-first: one 12–15 minute video a week ("what happens inside X when you Y"), with
Shorts cut from it plus invention and machine Shorts.

**Who it's for.** Curious people in the US, the UK, Australia and anywhere English is spoken: students
(computer science, electrical engineering, game development), working engineers, and retired computing
people. The comments under the niche's hits are full of experts who vouch for good explanations and
correct bad ones. We write for them: clear, never dumbed down, never skipping a step.

**What it covers.** How chips and computers work and how they are made; how AI models turn a question into
an answer, on real hardware, and what that costs; how the internet's physical machinery works (cables,
satellites, wifi, data centres); how everyday gadgets work inside; how famous inventions came to be; how
big tech machines (power plants, EVs, rockets) work.

**What it never covers.** Product reviews or buying advice; hacking or bypass how-tos; AI hype stated as
fact ("AI will replace…" predictions); rumours, leaks and unreleased products; chip politics or company
accusations beyond sourced facts; news for its own sake; medical or financial advice.

**Where facts come from.** Manufacturer documentation and press releases (TSMC, Intel, NVIDIA, ASML,
Apple, Samsung, SpaceX), standards bodies (IEEE, ITU, FCC), papers and university material, agencies (IEA
for energy, TeleGeography for cables), and reputable press. Every figure is a sourced fact copied unchanged
or computed by code, shown with its source, and checked by the fact review. Simplified drawings say so
("illustration", "not to scale", "3D recreation").

**Rules from the research.**
- Accuracy over spectacle: a wrong drawing is a factual error here; viewers catch both.
- Never skip the causal step (the most-praised hit was also the most criticised for skipping one).
- No footage, presenters or viral clips: recreate in 3D and tag it.
- No tell-tale AI script phrases; a calm, natural narrator; real depth.

**Disclaimer.** Animations are simplified illustrations, not exact to scale.
