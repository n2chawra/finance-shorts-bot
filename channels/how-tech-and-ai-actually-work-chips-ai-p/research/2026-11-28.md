| Short | Views | Subs | Outlier | Length |

- **The picture alone tells the cause.** Going by their descriptions: the solenoid pushes the small gear, the
  gear meshes with the flywheel, the flywheel turns the crank, the pistons move and the spark fires. Each step is
  a part visibly pushing the next one, in order and in one continuous cutaway. They need almost no words.
- **What ours do instead.** The contact sheet of L-20261127-21 (the ChatGPT long video the owner flagged) is
  mostly tiles, bars and word cards appearing on a stage. The operation itself is never shown happening: we
  don't see a token being multiplied or a word being picked. So the narration carries the "how", which is
  exactly the owner's complaint ("I have to hear things properly to understand rather than seeing").
- **Length:** these hits run 6–15 s, against our 45–60 s. They are single-mechanism loops, a different product
  from our explainers. `tech-length-35` already covers a shorter test, so I'm not proposing a new length test.
- **Voice:** two of the four have a voice and captions. The 280M one has no captions. No voice proposal: ours
  (am_michael, 75–100% of words narrated) is not where viewers leave.

## Doable changes, ranked
1. **`tech-show-the-mechanism`** (look challenger, `heldAfterHook`; it goes to the top of the look challengers
   because it answers an owner note). In Shorts where every beat makes sense with the sound off, the line's verb
   is acted out by parts in the scene (part A moves and pushes, carries or lights part B), and input → process →
   output is visible in one frame. Evidence: the four Shorts above, and the owner's note.
2. **`tech-long-show-the-mechanism`** (proposal, experiment, long videos): the same rule in the director's
   prompt for long videos, with the ChatGPT example spelled out (a token card enters the weight grid, rows light
   as it multiplies, a column of scores rises, the top one lifts out as the next word). Measure it on the next
   long video against L-20261127-21.
3. **Our own numbers:** the 3 best by average viewed (Crossrail 92.5%, NV1 74.4%, Paris Metro 71.3%) are all
   about one big machine or chip shown working. The 3 worst (microwave 33.9%, Hoover 46.9%, mobile phone 58.1%)
   open on an object or a person's story before the mechanism. That supports `tech-mechanism-by-6s`, which is
   already queued first among the first-second tests.
