## What top Shorts do that we don't
1. **We move the camera about three times as often.** In every Short we made since 20 Nov, each ~6 s beat has
   3 camera keys and 3 shot sizes, so the shot size changes about every 2 s, and the place changes on every beat
   (8–10 places a minute). The 13 reference Shorts hold a shot for 4–6 s with one camera move or none: 46% of
   their shots are static and 33% push in, with motion 1.5–1.9 out of 3. Branch Education often keeps one
   object across several shots. The `tech-calm-camera` look challenger (the owner's note) tests exactly this,
   so I added these numbers to its `why` instead of adding a second challenger.
2. **One machine, one place, and people stay.** Crossrail, the only Short that stays with one machine,
   kept 0.73 of its post-hook viewers to the end. The other three with curves kept 0.11 (microwave), 0.15 (dam)
   and 0.32 (transistor), and those change place every beat. Four Shorts isn't enough to call it, so I added the
   numbers to the reviewer's `tech-one-machine-one-place` challenger.
3. **Length: no case for a change.** The big on-profile Shorts in the scan run 84–144 s (NVIDIA's First Chip
   130 s, a Hindi chip story 144 s, a Tamil nuclear explainer 96 s), while Branch Education's run 56–60 s. That
   mix doesn't point either way. `tech-length-35` stays where it is.
4. **First drops are still at 5.6–7.5 s on a line that isn't the mechanism** (Crossrail 5.6 s "How? Its
   machines were 150 metres long", transistor 6.2 s). `tech-mechanism-by-6s` stays first among the first-second
   tests.
