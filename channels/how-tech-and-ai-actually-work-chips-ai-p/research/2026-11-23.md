## What top Shorts do that we don't
1. **The machine is working by the second line; history waits.** Both our curves lost their biggest share at
   7.5 s, on the line after the hook, and both times that line turned to the past: the microwave fell from 81% to
   52% of viewers on "In 1945, radar engineer Percy Spencer felt the chocolate in his pocket go soft", Hoover Dam
   from 100% to 81% on "Historically, it made up to 2,074 megawatts". The transistor Short has the same shape
   ("In 1947, John Bardeen and Walter Brattain built it by hand", third line); its curve isn't in yet. Crossrail's
   second line was the machine itself ("How? Its machines were 150 metres long, and each built the tunnel as it
   dug") and it is our only Short past 300 views. In the references, the biggest outlier, AI Tech 3.7's
   "How a Dam Generates Electricity" (8.7M views on 11K subscribers, 41 s), opens inside the intake with water
   moving and never gives a date; Inside Decoded's "This computer had no chips" gives the twist at 2.4 s ("it did
   maths with flowing water") and the inventor and year only at 5-12 s; Branch Education's "Zooming Into a CPU"
   goes from "billions of transistors" to "zooming in reveals an endless field" by 6 s. Routed as a first-second
   challenger, `tech-mechanism-by-6s`, placed first in its kind because it comes from our own drop points.
2. **Shorter.** The 40 on-profile Shorts with the most views a day in the scan have a median length of 31 s; ours are
   59-60 s and keep 11-17% to the end. Already on the challenger list (`tech-length-35`); nothing new to add.
3. **The second line is a twist, not context.** In 9 of the 12 reference Shorts the 3-9 s line is a "promise" or a
   contradiction ("yet dozens of countries still don't have it", "how? it is just air"), not background. Folded
   into the challenger above rather than a separate test.
4. Voice: nothing new; the references are single calm narrators at 150-190 words a minute, as ours.
