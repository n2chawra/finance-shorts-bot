## How each kind is treated

- **Stock and archive footage:** played as-is, cut every 3–5 s, no grade, no grain, no Ken Burns on
  footage. Old 4:3 TV ads sit **pillarboxed on a flat brand-coloured frame** (blue in the Gatorade
  video, red in Fazoli's) — the frame colour is the video's brand colour, used everywhere.
- **Photos:** slow push-in (about 5% over 5 s) on archive/B&W photos; store photos often static.
- **Charts:** Excel/PowerPoint-style bar charts — a dark grey rounded panel, white title ("2025
  Sports Drink U.S. Market Share"), thin gridlines, flat blue bars, category labels under them, the
  panel inset on the brand-colour frame. **No draw-on**: the 0.5 s burst at 50–52 s shows the chart
  cut in finished and held 7–10 s. The trick is repetition: the **same chart for two years** (2002 vs
  2026 locations; 2000 vs 2025 share) so the change is the story.
- **Documents:** a newspaper headline screenshot (big serif headline, byline, date) on the brand
  frame with a small "Source: <paper>" line bottom-left. Quotes: white bold sans paragraph on the brand
  colour, name right-aligned under it ("- <name>"). No highlighting or line-by-line lighting (unlike
  ColdFusion's lit sentence).
- **Logos:** cut-out on white, or composited over a waving national flag; product bottles cut out and
  slid in as split screens (orange half / blue half, each with its wordmark) for a "vs" video.
- **Big figure moment:** a huge white dollar figure sliding horizontally across stock footage
  (debt figure at ~560 s) — the only kinetic type seen.

## What appears on spoken words

Picture follows the noun of the sentence, cut on the word: brand named → its logo or bottle; "Coca-Cola"
→ the can; "market share" → the chart; "a group of doctors" → a B/W lab photo; "Olive Garden" → its
sign. Nothing animates *on* a word inside a shot (no pop-ins, no labels writing on); the change is
always a hard cut. Charts appear on the sentence that gives the number and stay through the next.

## Transitions

Hard cuts nearly always (probe: 23 picture changes a minute in the hook, only 3 of 25 on a sound hit).
One horizontal slide-in wipe for the split-screen title, one crossfade into the "VS" composite. No
whips, no zoom transitions.

## Text and labels (style only)

Heavy rounded geometric sans (Arial Black / Helvetica Bold feel), white on black or brand colour.
Chapter cards: one huge word ("1960s") filling 60% of the width on black, a product photo cropped at
the right edge, held ~5 s, hard cut in and out. Reason cards: white background, black heading with
the key word in red + underline, the company logo top-right, the reason in red below, food/product
cut-outs at both sides. No captions, no lower thirds of his own.

## Pacing

- ~187–190 spoken words a minute, steady, no pauses for effect.
- New picture every ~2.6 s in the hook (probe), every 4–6 s in the body; charts and cards hold 5–10 s.
- Length 11–12.5 min; ~2,100–2,300 words.

## Hook and structure

| | Gatorade vs. Powerade | Fazoli's |
|---|---|---|
| Frame 0 | sponsor logo (sponsor read 0–17 s), then 7 s ident | 7 s logo-montage ident |
| First line (gist) | the giant built a near-copy to beat the leader and never got close | it is a struggling, cheaper Italian fast-casual chain |
| Subject named | 24 s (first story second) | 7 s |
| First figure | spoken 45 s ("60–65%"), shown 53 s (chart) | shown 48 s (chart), spoken 59 s |
| Promise | 66–81 s: "decade by decade" | 117–124 s: "the history and five reasons" |
| Chapters | decade cards (1960s…2020s) | numbered reason cards (5 reasons) |
| Ending | summary + three questions to the comments, sponsor, thanks, silhouette end card | same, no sponsor |

The hook is a **contrast** with something the viewer knows (the giant that couldn't win; the chain
that once matched Chipotle), proven by one chart in the first minute, then a stated plan. Slow by
Shorts standards (subject at 7–24 s), but the channel's audience accepts it; the 7 s ident and the
sponsor-first open are the weakest parts and we should not copy them.

Read: a quiet, near-dry narration mix with a faint bed, no designed sound effects in the body, no
pre-reveal drops. For our channel: `bedLevel` low (~30–36 dB under the voice), `sfxPerMinute` long
≤3, effects only on chapter cards and the big figure, transition SFX off.

## Overall for our hybrid documentary

Company Man proves a ~1M-subscriber business-doc format with almost no motion design: stock +
archive + plain charts + cards, driven entirely by a fast, clear script with a contrast hook and a
numbered spine. Our edge over it is cheap to get: charts that draw on and morph between years,
documents with the spoken line lit, figure moments with count-ups — while keeping his discipline
(picture = the noun being said, hard cuts, one brand colour per video, chapter cards).

## Kit gaps

1. **Stock/archive footage player** (`FootageShot`): plays a licensed clip (Pexels/Pixabay/Wikimedia
   Commons video, or public-domain archive.org ads) with trim, optional slow push, and a
   **pillarbox frame** for 4:3 clips on the video's brand colour. Needs a footage fetcher with
   licence + credits (like `generator.images`), cached outside the repo.
2. **Brand-colour frame system**: one accent colour per video (from the subject's brand), applied to
   pillarbox frames, chart insets, quote cards, chapter cards.
3. **Bar chart with draw-on + year morph** (`BarCompare`): bars grow on the spoken figure, the same
   chart re-keys to a second year so bars shrink/grow in place; labels from `params.fig`.
4. **Headline / article card** (`HeadlineCard`): paper-style headline, byline, date, source line;
   push-in, optional highlight sweep on a phrase. Built as our own layout from sourced text — never a
   screenshot of a paywalled page unless the licence allows.
5. **Quote card** (`QuoteCard`): bold white quote on the accent colour, attribution under it, words
   lighting as spoken.
6. **Chapter card** (`ChapterCard`): huge one-word/year type on black with a product cut-out at one
   edge; numbered variant "Reason 3 of 5" with logo.
7. **Logo + flag composite** and **split-screen versus** (`VersusSplit`): two halves sliding in, each
   with packshot cut-out and wordmark (Commons logos only).
8. **Big figure slide** (`FigureSweep`): huge figure sliding across footage, value from `params.fig`.
9. **Sound pack**: a near-dry documentary mix preset (bed ~32 dB under the voice, no transition
   whooshes, one soft hit per chapter card), and a way to play a short archive clip's own audio.
