## Rise Fall Repeat: what the picture is

- **Picture kinds:** about 95% full-frame cinematic stills (YouTube labels the channel's videos
  "AI": generated period or boardroom scenes), about 3% a designed figure card, and about 2% a
  collage. No maps, no charts, no documents, no footage. **The look is the part we can't copy (no AI scenes).**
  The *grammar* carries over to our sources: one strong full-bleed image per sentence, people in it
  doing the thing the line says.
- **Treatment:** every still is graded the same way: warm teal-and-orange, crushed blacks, a hot
  practical light (window, lamp, furnace), shallow depth and a vignette. The camera is a constant slow
  push-in on every shot (motion 1). There is no pan, whip or shake. The probe's median frame change is
  2–4 per 50 ms tick, i.e. always drifting, never frozen.
- **One protagonist throughout:** the same founder in nearly every still (ageing or changing set),
  which works as a visual thread. The setting moves with the story: street → bench → boardroom →
  factory → modern HQ → the product today.
- **The figure card (Marvel, 97.7–105 s):** the one big result gets a movie-poster card: date on
  top, title in a large gold serif, a **huge gold "$585 MILLION"** with a small "WORLDWIDE / ON A
  $140 MILLION BUDGET" under it, over a cheering-crowd image. It appears in one cut (no count-up) and
  sits about 7 s. Every other figure lives only in the caption.
- **The ending picture:** YKK cuts to a modern glass tower (the company today), then an extreme
  close-up of a jeans zipper, then a six-panel collage of the product in everyday use. Marvel ends on
  a man on a mountain top at sunset (the "lesson" image).
- **What appears on the spoken words:** the pictures change on sentence boundaries, not on words.
  Only the caption is word-timed. 20 of the 33 cuts land on a sound hit (probe), so a cut usually
  comes with a low boom or whoosh.
- **Transitions:** 100% hard cuts. No wipes, no zoom transitions.

## Captions and text

- **Style:** 1–3 words at a time, bold geometric sans in ALL CAPS, white with a thin dark outline or
  shadow. One key word per chunk is yellow (a name, a figure, a verb such as "BRUTAL"). There is no box.
- **Size and position:** about 7–8% of the frame width per letter height (roughly 70–80 px on
  1080 × 1920). They sit centred at about 62–68% of the height, which keeps them clear of the Shorts UI.
- **Other text:** none, apart from the single figure card. There is no title bar, no lower third
  and no source line.

## The hook (pattern across the three)

1. **The title and the first line say the same thing.** Each opens on the paradox in the title, with
   the end figure up front ("built a $X empire" in the first 5 s, and the size isn't explained until
   the end). YKK instead opens with a year and a broke 20-year-old, and the title carries the promise.
2. **First frame:** the protagonist, full figure, in his world, in a mood (hero on a film set; salesman
   handing over a free package; broke youth in a muddy yard), with the first 2–3 caption words.
   There is no text card and no question.
3. **Never a question** in the opening line. The hook is a claim plus a paradox ("gave it away for
   free… built an empire"; "secret wasn't the zipper").
4. **Withholding the name** (Wrigley at 108 s) makes the name itself the reveal. It works when the
   brand is famous and the title hints at it without naming it.

## The ending

- **There is no loop.** Each ends on a spoken one-line "lesson" (2 of 3), then "follow for more
  business stories" (Marvel) or a turn onto the viewer's own clothes (YKK: "look at your zipper").
- The YKK ending is the one to copy: **the story lands on an object the viewer owns**, which
  matches our "thing in your hand" rule.

## Quick looks (skipped, but worth noting)

- **Signal 404 (Nokia 2.7M, bent iPhone 11.8M): a 10–14 s "text post".** A fixed paragraph of the
  whole story sits at the top in small white text with coloured key words, over a single clip
  of hands handling the product, with a meme-style line under it. There's no narration (music only),
  and it loops naturally because it is too short to finish reading. The huge views come from the
  object everyone remembers plus re-reads. **We could build it as our own format:** a still or
  photo of the product (Commons), the paragraph typed by code, and a synthesized bed. It could be a
  cheap second Shorts format to test, but it isn't the hybrid documentary.
- **ColdFusion aGLsZCeblcs:** the host's voice over 15 s of a small white text box on black, then
  someone else's vertical clip with green-highlighted captions. That's curation, not something we can make.

## Why these broke out

1. **A famous brand plus a paradox in the title** (bet on a nobody, gave it away free, the secret
   wasn't the product), with the outcome figure in the first 5 s.
2. **One underdog, one long arc**: misery stacked for 60–90 s, then a reversal, then the size of
   the empire, in short, flat, confident sentences.
3. **A cinematic picture every ~4 s with a slow push and an impact sound** on the cut. It feels like a
   film trailer, at a calm voice pace.
4. **Mute-proof captions** (1–3 words, a yellow key word, centred).

## Kit gaps (what we'd build for our hybrid version)

1. **`KenBurnsStill` for photos and stock** (`remotion/src/doc/`): a full-bleed image with a slow push
   (2–4% over the shot), an optional slow pan along a focus point, and the shared **teal-orange
   grade** (CSS filter / LUT-like overlay, vignette, film grain) so mixed sources (Commons photos,
   Pexels/Pixabay stock, company press images) look like one film. Size M.
2. **Stock and photo sourcing**: `generator/images.py` already fetches Commons. Add a **stock
   footage and still fetcher** for licence-clean sources (Pexels/Pixabay API, the no-download rule
   lifted for this channel) with credits recorded, plus a search by beat ("1930s workshop", "bank
   boardroom"). Size M. Period scenes (the 1890s, the 1930s) are the hard part without AI, so use
   archival Commons, Library of Congress and public-domain photos, and stock footage of period recreations.
3. **`PosterFigureCard`**: the movie-poster card (date, name, a giant gold serif figure from
   `params.fig`, a small subline with a second figure) over a darkened photo. Add an optional
   count-up. Size S.
4. **`ChunkCaptions`**: 1–3 word ALL CAPS chunks, white with an outline, one yellow key word
   chosen by code (a figure or the name), at about 64% height, about 75 px. This is close to our
   directed-video captions (big white words, figures in yellow), so it needs a chunk-size setting and
   a centre position. Size S.
5. **`ProductCollage`**: a 4–6 panel grid of the product in everyday use (stock), snapping in on the
   last line, for the "look at what you own" ending. Size S.
6. **Sound pack for this channel**: a dark cinematic bed generator (70–85 BPM, minor, sub-drone and
   low strings), an impact and boom set plus whooshes in `renderer/engine/audio.py`, an SFX hit on
   each cut, and a pre-reveal drop. These go in `channel.json` `sound` and are never borrowed from channel #1. Size M.
7. **Optional second format, `TextPostShort`**: a fixed paragraph on top with coloured key words
   over one product photo or stock clip, 10–14 s, music only, looping. Size S. A test candidate for `cycle/backlog.json`.
