# Build spec, part w1: KFC (owner's favourite) and two Modern MBA films (19 Nov 2026)

Watched in the built-in browser. Nothing was downloaded, and we used their techniques only, never their words or art.
- **hJTqDdf4jQc**: Bagholder Association, "KFC Won the World. So Why Is It Losing America?". 17:51 long, 591K views on a channel with 10.8K subscribers. We sampled the first 77 s every 2–3 s, then 10 points across the film and a 0.7 s burst on a document highlight. The audio probe ran over 0–77 s.
- **PU1zr8ka-0s**: Modern MBA, "Why Buffets Suddenly Disappeared". 38:33 long, 839K views. We sampled the first 60 s, 6 points across the film and a burst on a document.
- **BJ3PhlQ609w**: Modern MBA, "How Microsoft Wasted $113 Billion on Xbox". 45:50 long, 651K views. We sampled the first 60 s, 6 points across the film and a 4-frame burst on a chart card.

The sheets are in `research/sheets/<id>.json`. The shots after the first minute are spot samples, so per-video measures from the sheets (seconds per shot, figure share) only hold for the opening minute.

## 1. Mix of picture kinds (rough shares of running time)

| Kind | KFC (Bagholder) | Buffets (Modern MBA) | Xbox (Modern MBA) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stock / b-roll footage (food macros, store fronts, city streets, people eating) | **~60%** | ~5% | ~5% |
| Archival footage (old TV ads, news, launch events, period gameplay) | ~12% | **~80%** | **~80%** |
| Real photos (B&W founder photos, press photos) | ~5% | ~2% | ~2% |
| Documents and headlines (annual-report tables, article screenshots, social posts) | **~12%** | ~6% | ~2% |
| Charts / data cards | ~6% | ~2% | ~6% |
| Chapter title cards | ~2% (5 cards × 4–5 s) | none seen | none seen |
| Maps | none seen | none seen | none seen |
| Talking heads / narrator on camera | none | none | none |
| Sponsor read | none | ~3% (in-video, about 1 min) | ~2% |

**Overall:** all three films are made of footage with a voice over it. No one appears on camera and there is no animation. The owner's favourite (KFC) is the closest to our hybrid. Its mix is modern stock b-roll plus **documents as evidence** plus simple charts, with **chapter cards**. Modern MBA relies on **period archival** (old TV ads and news clips), which we can't license cheaply. Its lesson for us is "show the era", which we can do with Commons and public-domain photos and with period-styled graphics.

## 2. How each kind is treated

- **Stock footage (KFC):** clean, modern, well-lit b-roll with no added grade, grain or frame. It runs full-bleed 16:9 and cuts hard. Food is shown in macro and slow-motion close-ups. Store fronts are shown at night with neon. Camera moves come from the clips themselves (slow drift, follow); we saw no added Ken Burns on footage. A clip lasts 1–5 s. The opening montage runs about 0.7 s per clip (10.5–16.4 s), and the body runs 3–6 s per clip.
- **Archival footage (Modern MBA):** 4:3 VHS clips with their own grain and colour cast left as they are. They are **pillarboxed on a blurred, enlarged copy of the same clip**, so there are no black bars. A small white channel wordmark sits fixed top-right on everything. A clip runs 2–5 s. The old ads' own logos and on-screen text are left in as period flavour.
- **Real photos (KFC):** black-and-white archival portraits of the founder, full frame, with a **slow push-in** (Ken Burns at about 3–5% over 4 s) and no frame or border.
- **Documents (KFC; this is the signature):** a full-frame screenshot of a real page: an annual-report table, a trade-news article, a social post.
  - It sits on white with a slight paper-like softness and a slow push-in.
  - **On the spoken figure, a yellow highlighter wipes left to right across the exact sentence or table cell.** Burst at 323.5 / 324.3 / 325 s: there is no highlight at first, then about 25% covered, then the full line, so the wipe takes about 1.5 s and starts on the word.
  - In the first frame (0 s), a cell is already highlighted in a yellow box, and a second cell's highlight appears on its figure about 3 s later.
  - Social posts appear as cards that pop up and sit over a **blurred** screenshot of the post's page.
  - Modern MBA shows a newspaper clipping full frame, still, for 8+ s, with no highlight (weaker).
- **Charts:**
  - **KFC** uses plain light-grey, spreadsheet-like charts. Bold condensed caps carry the title and units on two lines. Thin pale-lavender bars, with the subject's bar in a deeper purple. A line chart's line **draws on left to right** and dives at the end. A slow push runs throughout.
  - **Modern MBA** uses a dark charcoal card with a **huge white one-word condensed title** ("SUBSIDIZED"-style) and a small spaced-caps subtitle. Bars go blue (price) up and red (loss) down from a zero line, with values in white bold on the bars. **Product cut-out photos sit as the x-axis labels.** The burst shows it **cuts in complete** and holds still for 5+ s, with no build.
- **Labels on footage (KFC):** a white box lower-left with heavy black text for one spoken spec (an oil temperature). It appears on the word and holds for the shot.
- **Chapter cards (KFC):** 4–5 s over a low-angle stock shot of the brand's building with its logo. The top line is a small red "CHAPTER N:" in heavy italic caps. Below it is the chapter name in large white heavy italic caps with a slight shadow. The narrator is silent and the **music drops** (the probe found a 15 dB drop at 66.6 s, during the card). There are 5 chapters, each named like a mechanism ("the trap", "the assault", "the franchise prison", "the blank slate", "the lessons").

## 3. What appears on spoken words

- A **figure is spoken → a document holding that figure is on screen and its sentence is highlighted on the word**. This is KFC's main device, used at 0 s, ~28 s, ~33 s and ~324 s. The figure itself is almost never typed big on screen. The proof is the page.
- A **rival or brand named → its product or store footage cuts in on the name.**
- A **comparison spoken → a chart** (KFC ~26 s chain ranking; ~868 s store count over time).
- A **process spoken → a label box** with the one number (the oil temperature on the fryer macro).
- **Modern MBA** cuts footage to the general topic, not to the word. Visuals sit loosely under the narration.

## 4. Transitions

Hard cuts everywhere. The probe counted **27 picture changes a minute** in KFC's first 77 s (about 2.2 s per picture; the montage pulls this up, the body runs 3–5 s). Only 8 of 35 cuts landed on a sound hit, so the cuts follow the voice, not the music. We saw no whips, wipes or zoom transitions. Chapter cards cut in and out.

## 5. Text, fonts and captions

- **Fonts (style only):** heavy condensed sans in caps for chart and card titles (an Anton / Bebas / Oswald-like weight), and heavy italic caps for chapter cards. Documents keep their own serif or sans. The channel's mark is a small two-letter monogram (KFC: bottom-right; Modern MBA: wordmark top-right) at about 60% opacity.
- **No burned-in captions in any of the three.** YouTube's CC appeared on KFC (auto, switched on by the player). Modern MBA has uploaded manual captions.
- On-screen text is rare: chapter cards, chart titles, one label now and then. The documents carry the reading.

## 6. Pacing

| | KFC | Buffets | Xbox |
|---|---|---|---|

| Words a minute | **~131 overall** (2,318 words; ~140–150 inside chapters) | ~165 | ~160–170 |

| Longest still | chapter card 5 s; documents 5–8 s with a push | clipping 8+ s fully still | chart card 5+ s fully still |

KFC is slow-spoken but cut fast. The calm voice over busy pictures is a contrast worth copying.

## 7. The hook

| | First frame | First line (gist) | Subject named | First figure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| KFC | an annual-report table with a highlighted cell | The brand's global size: countries, stores, sales | 1 s | shown 0 s, spoken 1 s |
| Buffets | 1980s buffet TV-ad footage | Places us in the buffet's golden decades | 0.5 s (the category) | spoken 4.5 s (a cheap set price) |
| Xbox | 2001 launch event, giant logo through smoke | Twenty years of console makers fighting | 0.5 s | the title carries the figure; first data card ~295 s |

KFC's hook structure runs in this order:
1. Global size, backed by the document (0–10 s).
2. It dominates abroad (fast montage).
3. "But at home…" (19 s).
4. The ranking drop with a chart and a highlighted article (26–36 s).
5. **The paradox**: demand is at a record, yet the brand lost (41–56 s).
6. The open question (56–63 s).
7. The Chapter 1 card at 64 s.

The subject is named in 1 s, the stakes are set by 30 s, and the question is asked at about 60 s.

## 8. Chapter structure and the ending

- **KFC:** a cold open (63 s), then 5 chapters of 2–4 min, each opened by a silent card. The chapters run:
  1. The origin, and how the habit shifted.
  2. Three rivals, one at a time, each with its per-store figure.
  3. Why it couldn't respond (the structure).
  4. The foreign contrast.
  5. The numbers, then three lessons.

  It ends with a two-part comment question, then a 20 s dark end screen with logo, subscribe and two video tiles. It doesn't loop.
- **Modern MBA:** no visible chapter cards. The story walks the eras chronologically. A 1-minute sponsor read sits mid-film. The film closes on a one-line verdict over archival footage into the end screen, with no comment ask.

| Brightness | 600 Hz centroid, bass share 13%, highs ~0 | dark, soft, low-mid: a cinematic/ambient pad with a soft pulse (inference) |

| Drops | **4 drops in 77 s**: 5.8 s (−17 dB, 1.2 s), 14 s (−13 dB), 66.6 s (−15 dB, 2.2 s, under the chapter card), 69.8 s (−17 dB) | the music pulls out before turns and under the chapter card |
| Silence | one 0.15 s full silence at 10.1 s (the cut from document to montage) | |
| Loudness range | 25 dB (p10 −39, p95 −14) | a dynamic mix, not a wall |

Modern MBA (not probed, per the owner's speed note): the bed sits under a natural human voice, and hardly any effects are noticeable on the frames.

**Settings for `channel.json` `sound`:**
- `bedLevel` puts the music about 22 dB under the voice. With duck 0.8, check on a Kokoro build; ~0.15–0.18 is likely.
- A dark ambient or cinematic pad at 70–85 BPM with almost no bright highs.
- `sfxPerMinute` long ~6.
- Effect kinds:
  - a soft low thud when a document or chart lands;
  - a paper/marker swish for the highlighter wipe;
  - a deep boom-and-cut before a chapter card.
- `preRevealDrop` {db 15, sec 1.5}, plus a full music drop under every chapter card.
- None of channel #1's ka-chings.
- Narration about 140–160 words a minute (slower than our 215 long pace).

## 10. Kit gaps (what we'd build)

1. **`DocumentShot`** (Remotion): shows a page image (a PDF page render, SEC filing, press release, Commons scan or our own typeset re-creation of a public report) full frame on off-white with a soft paper shadow and a 3–5% push-in. Takes `highlights: [{rect|textRange, at: word}]` and draws a **yellow marker wipe** (multiply blend, slightly ragged edges, 1.2–1.6 s, left to right) starting on the spoken word. Optional `focus` blurs the rest of the page and lifts one paragraph. Figures stay in `params.fig`; the highlighted figure must match the sourced fact (number check).
2. **Our own page renderer** for articles and tables: we can't screenshot news sites' pages and logos freely, so we typeset neutral "article", "filing table" and "post card" templates. They carry the real sourced sentence, attribution line ("Source: <outlet>, <date>") and our own styling. Social posts become our own neutral card style over a blurred page backdrop. **Asset sources:** SEC EDGAR filings (public domain), company press releases (quote and cite), Commons scans.
3. **`ChartCard`** in two looks:
   - **light**: grey grid, thin bars, the subject's bar in the accent colour, a line that draws on;
   - **dark**: a huge one-word condensed title, bars up and down from zero in two colours, values on the bars, **product/logo cut-outs as axis labels**.

   Bars and lines build on the spoken word, which KFC and MBA don't do; that's our edge. Numbers come from `maths` / facts only.
4. **`ChapterCard`**: a full-frame stock or photo background with a slow push, a small red "CHAPTER N:" over a large white heavy-italic title, 4–5 s, silent, with a music drop. The director marks chapters in the script.
5. **`FootageShot`**: plays a licensed stock clip with `trim`, `speed`, an optional `kenBurns` on stills, and **`pillarbox: "blur"`** for 4:3 or vertical sources (a blurred, scaled copy behind). Optional `grade` presets ("neutral", "archival": slight desaturation, grain, gate weave) so old photos sit in their era.
6. **`LabelBox`**: a white box with heavy black text, lower-left, appearing on its word over footage (one spec or figure).
7. **Stock-footage pipeline** (the no-download rule is lifted for this channel only): a licensed source with an API, such as Pexels or Pixabay (free licence, no attribution needed), or Storyblocks/Artgrid if paid. Add a fetch step like `generator.images` that records clip id, author, licence and URL in `credits.json`, and caches under `E:/cache/`, never committed. We need search terms per beat from the director (store fronts at night, food macros, kitchens, city streets, offices, stock tickers).
8. **Archival substitute**: we can't use old TV ads. Use **Commons / public-domain photos** (founders, early stores, products), Library of Congress and National Archives images, and early-catalogue scans with the archival grade and a push-in. Where none exist, use a period-styled motion graphic (our flat kit with a grain/sepia pass).
9. **Fonts**: one heavy condensed display face (OFL, e.g. Anton or Bebas Neue) and a heavy italic for chapter cards. Both need the owner's OK, since a download is needed.
10. **Watermark**: a small corner monogram at ~60% opacity, plus a 20 s end-screen layout (dark card, logo, two video slots, subscribe) for long videos.
11. **Sound**: a new "documentary dark pad" bed (70–85 BPM, low-mid, slow pulse, no highs), effect kinds (low doc-land thud, marker swish, pre-chapter boom), and a chapter-card music drop.

Not ours, and left out: other channels' footage, logos used as decoration beyond identification, and auto-highlighted screenshots of paywalled news pages.
