## Why these small channels broke out

- **The subject is a thing the viewer owns or has seen this year** (a viral plush toy, a tumbler, the
  phone they had as a kid), not a company's annual totals. The title asks the question the viewer
  already has ("what happened to X", "why everyone bought X and stopped", "who killed X"). Big
  channels lead with scale; these lead with recognition.
- **A full rise-and-fall arc inside the first 60 s**, then the promise. Labubu: dated auction scene
  and price (0–14 s), "worth more than the giants" (14–30 s), the crash (30–48 s), "no rival did it,
  one textbook decision did" (48–61 s). Stanley: the cup in your car (0–16 s), sold out, tenfold
  sales, stampedes (16–53 s), the cool-down (53–67 s), "it wasn't luck" (67 s). The viewer gets the
  whole shape early; the body sells the *why*.
- **A named mechanism as the payoff** (scarcity was the product; making more killed it), stated in
  plain words ("In plain English: the best year ever"), and a lessons section at the end.
- **Every claim looks sourced**: source chips ("ESTIMATE" with the outlet's logo), article
  screenshots with one sentence highlighted, agency footage credits, analyst notes as paper cards.
- **The subscribe pitch sits right after the promise** (61–76 s Labubu, ~110 s Stanley) and again
  before the "what next" section. A title card ("The Toy That Was Worth More Than ___") closes the
  cold open.
- Q Mobile: nostalgia + whodunit title in a language market with few such channels; the craft is
  cheap (host to camera), the topic and title did the work.

## How each kind is treated

**Footage.** Full frame, natural grade, no grain, no frames. Slow push or none; credits in tiny grey
type bottom-right ("Image: …", agency logo). Footage is the bed under big type: date slams, a
place label (city in bold caps, venue in small caps, lower-left), a name lower-third (bold caps name,
small title line), a "CITY · YEAR" chip top-left, a red caption tag ("shares: more than half gone").
When a figure lands over footage, the footage dims ~40% and blurs slightly (Stanley: rack-focus from
the shelf to a product cut-out in front).

**Photos.** Two treatments. (1) A **polaroid**: B&W, white border, tilted 3–8°, soft drop shadow,
on a charcoal or paper card; several in a row, each **unblurs on its spoken name** (26–30 s). A
footage shot can **freeze into a polaroid** that slides aside for the next object (60 s). (2) A
**die-cut product**: a clean cut-out of the product on the channel's backdrop, sliding in, lining up
in rows, filling a pallet grid (5,000 units drawn as a grid of tops), a wall of colour variants.

**Backdrops (one per channel, the brand).** Labubu: charcoal (#1d1d1d-ish) cards and an off-white
**paper texture** card; Stanley: a **blue blueprint grid**. Every graphic lives on the backdrop, so
footage, photos and charts read as one set.

**Figures.** Always a designed object, never plain text:
- **count-up** over dimmed footage (¥0 → 1,080,000 in ~2 s, mint, bold sans, italic serif unit line
  under it);
- a **phone resale-listing card** whose price **flips** to the new price, then a red "-88%" tag slams
  in at an angle;
- a **split-flap ticker board** ("TICKER · EXCHANGE", 3 digits flip down), with a glossy 3D icon
  (piggy bank) that **cracks apart**, and a black label-maker tape "-60% FROM PEAK";
- huge orange "-22.5%" with a small caps line above and a white zigzag line drawing down beside it;
- serif italic year + serif figure for Stanley's "2019 $73M → 2023 $750M".

**Charts.** Horizontal **bar with logos** (the subject's long mint bar, rivals' bars stacking under
it in grey, each with its logo); a **bar that sinks and leaves a dashed outline of its old height**
(38% → 14%); a **donut** filling to a share; a line that **draws on** and dips with the label at its
end. Charts animate on the figure's word; values sit on the bar end.

**Documents.** A paper card with a serif quote that **types on line by line**, a small caps
attribution ("ANALYST NOTE", a name in caps), and **one key phrase in a mint highlight box**. Real
article screenshots get a **yellow highlighter swipe** over one sentence and an outlet logo chip;
slow push in. A typewriter receipt ("RESALE PLATFORM · DATE · ITEM") for a single transaction.
Q Mobile: a newspaper clipping with a slow push (the classic Ken Burns headline).

**Maps.** Dark textured continent, the relevant countries light up; a typewriter tape label ("WITHIN A
WEEK"). Used once, as a beat, not a chapter.

**Chapter cards.** Black **label-maker tape** text ("2024 · THE MONSTER") on white paper; chapters
also in YouTube's chapter list (9 chapters in 16 min, ~1.8 min each).

**Icons.** Glossy 3D emoji-style objects (trophy, piggy bank, flames on a car cut-out) used sparingly,
one per beat, with a simple action (crack, burn).

## What appears on spoken words

- The date or place appears **on its words** as a big title over footage (0.5 s after the line starts).
- A figure **counts up while it is spoken** and settles on the unit line ("1.08 million yuan").
- Named things appear **one per name**: three rival brands unblur in turn; three phone cards in a row.
- The fall lands **as a second motion on the same object**: the price flips, the bar sinks, the
  ticker rolls down, the piggy bank breaks.
- The idea word gets a **highlight**: "one decision" in a mint box; "Barbie" boxed in the title;
  "slot machine mechanics" highlighted in the quote.
- Between beats the backdrop stays and objects slide off/on (a set, not a slideshow).

## Transitions

Mostly **hard cuts** (Labubu 18 picture changes in 54 s, one every ~3 s), objects sliding in/out on
the same backdrop, a freeze-to-polaroid, a rack-focus blur. Stanley fades in from black at 0 s. No
whips, no glitch, no zoom transitions. **Cuts are not hit with a sound** (0 of 18 cuts had a hit).

## Text / label style (style only)

- Bold geometric sans (Poppins/Inter-like, heavy) for titles and figures, white or mint.
- **Italic serif** for asides and units ("roughly", "for a toy.", "1.08 million yuan"); Stanley uses a
  high-contrast serif for all figures and years (gold/white).
- Small tracked caps for eyebrows and labels ("MARKET VALUE", "POP MART · LABUBU").
- Accent: one channel colour (mint green on charcoal; gold/yellow on blue), red only for losses.
- Label-maker tape and typewriter type for chapter cards and receipts.
- **No burned-in captions** in any of the three.

## Pacing

- Labubu ~146 words/min overall (pauses included), Stanley ~170, Q Mobile ~170 (Hindi).
- A new picture every ~3 s in the body (probe: 19.9 cuts/min); in the cold open every 1–4 s.
- Graphic beats hold 5–10 s with motion inside them (count, flip, sink); footage shots 2–5 s.
- 16 min and 14.5 min: long for "small" channels; the full arc in 60 s pays for the length.

## Chapter structure (Labubu, 9 chapters)

Cold open (0–1:18) → origins (creator, then the retailer) → how the machine made desire (three
named layers: gamble, scarcity, resale) → the decision (make more) → the collapse (resale prices,
stock) → the money (results vs share price) → the comeback plan → lessons (three numbered, "our
view", with a "this is opinion" line) → comment prompt + subscribe + disclaimer. Stanley: same
spine, ends by **looping back** to the three bloggers and the car cup holder from the opening.

## Ending

Labubu: asks viewers for their own story in the comments, three lessons, subscribe ask, an
educational disclaimer. Stanley: callback to the opening scene and a forward question (what will be
rationed next). Neither uses an end-card montage; YouTube end screen only.

Stanley and Q Mobile not probed (owner's speed note). By ear-free inference from the transcript's
[music] marks, Stanley's music swells only in gaps between paragraphs. Q Mobile's host track
carries more effects (estimate ~10 a minute) and is not our target.

**Our sound target:** a near-silent, dark, slow bed (≈35 dB under the voice, little bass, no kick),
music up only in the cold open and between chapters; ~5 effects a minute, soft bright ones on a
figure flip/slam, a tag landing or a document appearing; nothing on plain cuts; no money "ka-ching".

## Kit gaps (what we would build)

1. **Footage player** with Ken Burns (0.5–1.5%/s push), dim + blur on demand, credit line slot, and a
   freeze-frame-to-polaroid transition. Sources: Pexels/Pixabay video (licence-checked), Wikimedia
   Commons video, agency footage only where licensed (the hybrid's lifted no-download rule).
2. **Overlay set on footage**: date slam, place label (city + venue), name lower-third, "CITY · YEAR"
   chip, red caption tag, source chip (outlet logo + "ESTIMATE"/"REPORT").
3. **Polaroid photo**: B&W/colour, tilt, shadow, unblur-on-word, row of N with per-name reveal.
4. **Product cut-out stage**: die-cut product images (our own renders or Commons cut-outs) that slide,
   line up, fill a pallet/wall grid of N units (count from `maths`).
5. **Channel backdrop**: one per channel (charcoal + paper texture card, or a blueprint grid), with
   paper grain texture.
6. **Figure objects**: count-up with italic unit line; **phone listing card** with price flip + loss
   tag; **split-flap ticker**; big percent + drawing zigzag line; donut; bar with logos; **sinking bar
   with dashed ghost outline**. All values from `params.fig`/`maths`.
7. **Document kit**: quote card typing on with a highlight box on the key phrase; article
   screenshot frame with a yellow highlighter swipe on one sentence and an outlet chip; typewriter
   receipt; newspaper clipping with push. (Article screenshots are third-party: rebuild them as our
   own typeset cards citing the source, not captures.)
8. **Label-maker tape** chapter card and typewriter labels (needs a mono/typewriter OFL font).
9. **Italic serif** OFL font (e.g. a Playfair/Libre Caslon-style) for asides and figures.
10. **3D emoji-style icons** with one action (crack, burn): a small library of our own drawn icons.
11. **Dark textured map** with countries lighting up (reuse the geography channel's `WorldMap`).
12. **Sound**: a dark ambient bed preset at −35 dB under voice, swell-only-between-chapters automation,
   and an SFX set of soft clicks/flips/tag slams, none on cuts.
