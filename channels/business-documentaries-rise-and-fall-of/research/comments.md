# Comment read: business documentaries (rise and fall)

Read 2026-11-19 in the built-in browser, Top comments. All paraphrased; no usernames.

**Coverage (owner stopped the read early):** Labubu (~240 top comments) and Pepsi (~160). KFC loaded but
was not read; Gatorade vs Powerade and buffets were not opened. So the counts below come from two
videos only. Treat them as a direction, not a measurement.

**Caveat:** almost nobody wrote "do X next". Viewers instead name *comparable* brands and fads
("this is Beanie Babies again", "they forgot the Harrier jet"). The list below is those mentions:
they show which stories the audience already knows and connects. That is a good sign of demand, but
it is not the same as a request.

Themes that run through it all: **hype and artificial scarcity** (scalpers, blind boxes as gambling,
celebrity endorsement, Lisa and Rihanna), **a recipe change or price rise that drove loyal buyers away**,
and **"I saw this coming"**: viewers like feeling they predicted the fall.

## 2. What viewers praised or complained about

**Praised**
- Storytelling and editing ("great breakdown", "underrated", "great analysis", "congrats on the huge
  video"); one viewer praised the editor's cuts.
- Opening hooks with a concrete, surprising figure: a viewer recalled the Labubu cold open (a huge
  figure sold for about $150K, then a secret edition losing most of its value in six months) as what
  stuck with them.
- Funny or odd visual metaphors in the illustrations were singled out as memorable.
- A clear verdict on the business ("built on hype, so it fades").

**Complained**
- **Missing facts:** Pepsi viewers listed what was left out (sweetener change, Harrier jet, Number Fever,
  Pepsi Max, Walmart pricing). Ex-insiders (a merchandiser, a production controller, a distributor) said
  the video only scratched the surface or that sales were actually up.
- **US-centric framing:** many said Pepsi is fine or dominant outside the US, and that Dr Pepper does not
  exist in their country. A global audience notices when a story assumes the US.
- **Errors spotted at timestamps:** a wrong item circled at 1:41; a weak Disney comparison at 13:05;
  a scalper's quote used as evidence was mocked as thin sourcing.
- **AI narration suspicion:** one viewer congratulated the creator but said they were fairly sure it was
  an AI narrator. That was the only mention of the voice.
- **Length:** one person asked for a short version; nobody else mentioned length.
- No comments seen about music, ads/sponsors or political bias in these two.

## 3. Language and countries

- Almost all comments are in English. One was in Chinese (saying the narration was easy to follow without
  subtitles), so non-native English speakers are watching too.
- Countries named in the comments: US (many: Washington, Baltimore, Arizona, Orlando), UK, Germany (3+),
  Poland (2), Sweden, Norway, the Netherlands, Australia (2), Canada (Quebec), India (3), Philippines (2, incl.
  balikbayan boxes), Vietnam, Thailand, Southeast Asia generally, Turkey, Mexico (cane-sugar Pepsi), Latin America,
  China.
- So the audience is a **global English-speaking one led by the US, with a large European and South/Southeast
  Asian share**. Prices given in a viewer's own currency (PLN, EUR, INR) come up often: per-country
  price comparisons would land well.
