# Champion recipe: Business documentaries

The writer and the director follow this over the shared prompts' defaults (money Shorts,
the 28–35 s length, the flat cast, directed 2D beats). The hard rules do not change: true, sourced facts;
figures from code or a sourced `facts` entry; one narrator; no real person's words in the voice. Sources:
`research/advice.md`, `research/report.md`, `research/style.json`, `research/families.json`,
`style-study/2026-11-19-references.md` (the build spec). Long-first: the long video is the channel.

## The story

- **Start from a thing the viewer owns or has seen** (a toy, a cup, a soda, a phone, a store sign), never
  a company's totals. The breakouts all did (Labubu, Stanley, Pepsi, KFC).
- **Topic shares, long (per 8):** product boom and bust 3 (1.69×, 21% hits); brand backlash 2 (29% hits,
  one channel posting it: a gap); company rise and fall 2 (1.37×); business war 1 (205,163 median from
  only 15 videos).
- **Topic shares, Shorts (owner, 2026-11-28, from our own first 17 Shorts):** a thing the viewer owns or a
  brand they know, in a boom and bust (Tamagotchi 1,500, Stanley 1,144), a rise and fall (Krispy Kreme 1,541,
  Nokia 700) or a business war (Toyota vs Mercedes 1,282). Founder stories at most one Short in four: ours had a
  median of 62 views (Nike, FedEx, Spanx, Dollar Shave Club, Lipton), because they are the stories every faceless
  business channel retells every week. Every Short is a cut-down of a long video's best minute when one exists.
- **Untold beats famous (owner, 2026-11-28).** Never the textbook version of a story (Nike's $35 logo, FedEx's
  blackjack night, Airbnb's cereal, Adidas and Puma's brothers, Spanx's $5,000): dozens of channels tell those each
  week and YouTube stops showing a new copy. Pick a famous brand's **less-told** turn (a recall, a price change, a
  forgotten product, a deal that fell through) or a craze from TV and malls that few channels have done (Ronco,
  BowFlex). `research/saturation.json` counts the same-story videos of the last 7 and 30 days; the planner refuses
  a crowded topic.
- **Avoid as the topic:** corporate scandal and fraud (0.86×; legal risk), finance deals and private
  equity (Shorts 0.59×, 0 hits in 51), how a business makes money (crowded, 0.87×). A scandal belongs
  inside a rise and fall.
- **Settled and attributed:** every claim rests on a filing, court record, regulator finding, company
  report or named outlet, shown on screen. Quotes appear as text, never in the narrator's voice.
- **The global beat:** one beat on how the story went outside the US when it differs (Pepsi in Europe,
  Quebec, India), with a price in the local money where it helps. Viewers complain about US-only framing.

## Long videos: 15–25 minutes (900–1,500 s)

### The first 60 seconds (the arc)

1. **Frame 1 is the object** (the toy at an auction, the cup going into a car) **or the document** with
   the figure already highlighted. Never a logo ident, a sponsor or a black card.
2. **The subject named by 1 s** (references: 0.5 s median).
3. **A figure before 10 s** (7 s median), as an object: a count-up, a price flip.
4. **The whole rise and fall by 50 s**: the boom fast, the fall, the paradox ("everyone bought it, then
   nobody did").
5. **The promise of one cause** by ~65 s: "it wasn't the rival, it was one decision."
6. **Title or chapter card** right after, with a music drop.

### The body

- **5–9 chapters**, each opening on a 4–5 s chapter card: a small red "CHAPTER N" over a big heavy-italic
  title on a slowly pushing still, the music dropping under it, a low thud.
- The payoff (the one cause, proven) at about 38% of the runtime, then what it cost, the global beat,
  and what happened next.
- ~210 words a minute (voice speed 1.33; owner 2026-11-19: 1.5x the references felt right), short pauses before reveals.

### The ending

The mechanism in plain words ("scarcity was the product"), a short lessons section (2–3 lines), one
question for the comments, and a 20 s end screen. No subscribe ask in the cold open beyond one line.

## The document device (the signature)

When a figure or claim is spoken, **the page that holds it is on screen**: an annual-report table, a
filing, a court document, a press release. Off-white page, soft shadow, a 3–5% push to the line, then a
**yellow highlighter wipe across the exact sentence on the word** (1.2–1.6 s); the rest of the page
blurs. News articles and social posts are **rebuilt as our own typeset cards** with the real sourced
sentence and "Source: <outlet>, <date>", never a screenshot. 15–20% of a long video.

## Figures as objects

A spoken figure becomes a thing that moves twice: a count-up over dimmed footage; a resale listing whose
price flips with a red "−88%" tag; a split-flap ticker rolling down; a bar that sinks and leaves a dashed
ghost of its old height; logo bars; a donut. Never just a number typed big. Values only from `params.fig`.
Red only for losses; one accent colour otherwise.

## The picture mix (long)

| Kind | Target |
|---|---|
| Stock footage (Pexels, Pixabay, Commons video) | 35–45% |
| Archival photos (Commons, LoC, public domain), archival grade | 15–20% |
| Documents and headline cards | 15–20% |
| Motion graphics (figures, charts, maps, timelines) | 20–25% |
| Person on camera, cast, AI scenes | 0 |

- **Every shot moves**: footage pushes or drifts 0.5–1.5%/s, stills Ken Burns. Hard cuts only, every
  ~2.2 s in the hook and 3–5 s after, on the noun spoken.
- **One grade over every source**, one backdrop (charcoal paper or grey grid). Old 4:3 or vertical
  sources sit on a blurred copy of themselves.
- **A name spoken → its photo arrives** (a tilted B&W polaroid that unblurs). Overlays: date slam, place
  label, lower-third, source chip ("REPORT" / "FILING" + outlet), red tag.
- No identifiable stock person on a negative beat; logos only to identify.

## Shorts: 30–55 s

- **The title and the first spoken line state the same paradox**, as a claim, never a question ("He gave
  away his product. It built a billion-dollar company.").
- **The big figure by 5 s**; the subject named by 1 s.
- One full-frame picture per sentence, a constant push, hard cuts every ~3–4 s (first cut by 3 s), one
  grade over everything.
- **The one big result** gets a gold poster card (giant gold figure, the cost in a subline) for ~5–7 s;
  every other figure only in the captions.
- **Captions**: 1–3 words at a time, bold white capitals, the key word yellow, no box, ending at least
  340 px above the bottom edge.
- ~210 words a minute (voice speed 1.35; owner 2026-11-19: 1.5x): 105–190 words.
- **The ending ties to the viewer's own life** ("look at the zip on your jacket"), over the product in
  everyday use. It need not loop.

## Sound

The channel's own beds and effects (`channel.json` `sound`): a dark, slow, low-mid bed well under the
voice (~32 dB in long videos), up in the cold open and under chapter cards; soft effects only on things
arriving (a page landing, the highlighter, a price flip, a ticker), a thud on chapter cards, nothing on
plain cuts; Shorts put a low boom on most cuts and drop the music for ~0.6 s after the hook and before
the reveal.

## Words

- Plain, journalistic, certain where the record is; attribute everything else ("the company's 2019
  filing says", "a court found").
- No "Did you know", no "Watch this", no hype, no AI script tics ("delve", "let's dive in").
- Never mock the people involved; no graphic detail of deaths or injuries.
