# Going Down

**What it is.** A Shorts-only channel, three a day, 20–25 seconds each. Every Short asks one question, "What's under
this famous place?", and answers it with one continuous camera move: from the landmark above ground straight down
through every real layer beneath it (subway lines, tunnels, vaults, secret rooms, foundations, the water table,
bedrock) to the deepest or most surprising point, with a depth meter counting the feet and metres. A pull-back shows
the whole stack at once, and the camera rides back up so the last frame is the first and the Short loops.

**Who it's for.** Curious people anywhere in the world who like "I never knew that was down there". The picture
carries the story, so it works on mute and for viewers whose first language is not English: the depth figures, the
station-style signs and the cutaway read in any language. The scan (21 Nov 2026) found the demand: a two-month-old
channel's "What's Under the Lakers' Court?" reached 1.36M views, a secret bunker under a house 6.2M, and "How deep
is the ocean" 45.6M. Nobody else makes the pure 20–25 s dive.

**What it covers.** Famous stations, landmarks, towers, stadiums, religious sites, dams, canals, tunnels and whole
cities, worldwide (about a third outside the US and UK). Never: places whose layers can't be sourced, military or
security details beyond what is officially published, anything that helps someone get into a restricted place,
private homes, tragedies told for shock.

**Where facts come from.** Every depth, length and time is a sourced fact: the operator or owner (MTA, TfL, a metro
company, a dam authority), engineering bodies and journals (ASCE, ICE), governments, and Wikipedia only where it
cites a primary source. A layer whose depth isn't published is shown without a number, never guessed. When sources
disagree the most official one wins and the others are noted for the fact reviewer. Metres and feet are converted in
code. Legends ("Nazi saboteurs", "secret lizard tunnels") are said as legends or left out.

**Rules of the field.** Cutaways are illustrations, not to scale, and say so in the description. Line colours and
bullets of transit systems are drawn in our own style, never copied logos. Real photos only from Wikimedia Commons
with credit. No real person speaks.
