# Disaster Desk

**Who it is for:** a global English-speaking audience (the US, UK, Europe, India and Australia lead the niche's comments)
who want to understand how real machines and structures fail: planes, trains, ships, bridges, dams, buildings, rides,
spacecraft and industrial plants. They are curious and technical. The most-liked comments under the niche's hits explain
the physics themselves, or point out that safety rules are "written in blood". They come for the cause, not the gore.

**What it covers:** true stories of failures that have an official investigation behind them. Each one is told minute by
minute as a cause chain: how the thing was meant to work, the corners cut or small decisions that added up, the moment
it failed (reconstructed, with the failing part shown), and what changed afterwards. That might be the rule, the standard
or the design fix. Long videos are 8–10 min, one every 48 hours. Each has one promo Short cut from its moment of
failure. A recent major crash takes the next slot through the fast lane, labelled preliminary, with a follow-up when
the preliminary report lands.

**What it never covers:** gore, bodies, injuries or victims' close-ups; "horror" framing; blame beyond what the official
report says; speculation about an open investigation; natural-disaster explainers (0.66× in the scan); fiction or
invented examples; weapons how-tos; living people's private details. Real people's words (cockpit recordings, radio,
witnesses) appear as on-screen transcript text, never in the narrator's voice.

**Where facts come from:** official investigation reports and public inquiries (NTSB, AAIB, RAIB, MAIB, BEA, ATSB, TSB,
CSB, NASA and ESA boards, national commissions), then court records and reputable outlets. Every figure on screen comes
from a sourced `facts` entry with its URL, or is computed by code. US government reports and their diagrams are
public domain. Photos come from Wikimedia Commons under free licences, with credits in the description. Never another
channel's footage.

**The rules of its field:**
- A realistic 3D shot of a real event carries "3D reconstruction · not actual footage" on screen, and the upload uses
  YouTube's synthetic-content disclosure.
- Deaths are stated plainly, once, with respect.
- A recent event is labelled preliminary until the final report.
- The disclaimer is in every description.

**The look:** every video combines all three kits: type-accurate 3D for the moment of failure, flat engineering
diagrams and cause chains, and the real report page with its finding highlighted. Each video draws a different look
recipe from the rotation (never one of the last 4), so no two videos look alike (owner, 20 Nov). The channel's
identity stays constant: mono labels, the honesty tags, and red always meaning the failure.

**Risks, as rules:**
1. The niche's bottom is flooded with low-effort channels, so no video ships unless its first minute beats the references.
2. Inauthentic content: original scripts and a new look every video.
3. Limited ads on tragedy: no gore, no sensational thumbnails.
4. Defamation: the report's words only.
5. Cloud render time: 3D only at the key 60–120 s.
