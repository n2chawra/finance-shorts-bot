# What viewers say (20 Nov 2026)

The scan returned no comments, so I read the top comments under two hits in the browser: Fascinating Horror's Eastern Airlines 212
(1,974 comments) and Fault Lines' Florida Learjet 3D reconstruction (1,945). They are paraphrased here, never quoted at length.

## Themes (most-liked first)

1. **"Rules are written in blood."** The most-liked kind of comment ties the disaster to the rule it created (the sterile
   cockpit rule). Viewers love an ending that names the rule, check or design change that came out of it.
   → Every video ends on "what changed", named and dated.
2. **The twist in the numbers.** A viewer reacted to "about 50 were alive after the crash" being followed by
   "only 10 survived". A figure that turns is the strongest emotional beat.
   → Hold the survivor or death figure for a turn; never put it in the title.
3. **Human moments.** Crew who pulled a colleague out, a passenger's last words, the luck that nobody on the ground
   was hit. → One named human moment per video, told with respect (text on screen, never voiced).
4. **Viewers explain the cause themselves.** Long, highly liked comments explain tailplane icing and overspeed in
   detail, and a former B-52 commander weighs in. The audience wants **real technical depth**, explained
   step by step.
   → A clear "how it physically failed" beat, with the 3D cutaway at the moment of failure.
5. **"How fast it all changed."** Seconds-long timelines grip: "the last 60 seconds", "26,000 feet in about a minute".
   → A clock or altitude counter on screen during the failure.
6. **Blame questions.** Viewers ask why the crew flew into the storm and who should have known. We keep to what
   the report says.
7. **Links to famous people** (Stephen Colbert's father and brothers died on Eastern 212) draw big replies.
   → Add a sourced connection when there is one.
8. **Follow-ups.** Fault Lines pinned a promise to post again when the NTSB preliminary report lands. Recent crashes
   become a two-part story: now, and when the report comes out.

## Demand signal seen under the videos

The Learjet 60 crash of 1 Nov 2026 pulled huge numbers across channels within 3 weeks: Captain Steeeve 8.3M,
Flying for Money 3.1M and 2.8M, Fault Lines 1.9M, Mentour Now 917K, GeographicUnited 368K. **A recent crash,
reconstructed fast and accurately, is the biggest single opportunity in the niche.** It needs a fast lane
(published within days, from official statements and ADS-B/ATC data) and the on-screen label
"3D reconstruction · not actual footage".

## Topic ideas in viewers' words (paraphrased)

- What did the pilot say in the last minute?
- Could any pilot have saved it?
- Why didn't they fly around the storm?
- What was she trying to tell the captain?
- The rule that exists because of this crash
- How did nobody on the ground die?
- The flyover / bridge that was built with every corner cut
- A train driver who couldn't hear the radio warnings
- The brand-new dam that failed
- They never thought to check (a collapse from a skipped inspection)
