got 1.94M views on a 10-minute 3D crash reconstruction. The long trend is rising. But a wave of new low-effort
channels in the same niche sits at 30–300 views, so this channel works only at high craft, which matches the
owner's rule: gripping or nothing.

## Doable by us

- **Look:** every kit in every video. 3D for the moment of failure, flat 2D for maps, routes, timelines and cutaways,
  the doc kit for real photos, report pages, diagrams and headlines.
  **Build items:** a look-rotation and novelty gate (the owner's no-repeat rule); 3D set pieces (plane, ship, train,
  bridge, building); a cockpit/radio transcript prop; a route path tool on the map.
- **Render:** cloud 3D is about 3.75 s a frame, so 3D covers only 60–120 s per video, or those beats render on the PC.
- **Voice:** a calm Kokoro documentary narrator. Real people's words (cockpit recordings, witnesses) appear as
  transcript text, never voiced.
- **Policy:** original research and scripts, with looks varied per video (inauthentic content is judged channel-wide).
  US government reports are public domain. Photos come from Commons with credits. A realistic reconstruction gets the
  synthetic-content label.

## Conditions

1. Craft before volume: no sample ships unless it beats the references' first minute.
2. A no-repeat look gate, with every kit mixed in each video.
3. **10–15 min long videos** (the data's best band; 5–6 min is the weakest), one every 48 h, plus one promo Short each.
4. 3D only at the key moments.
5. Settled, sourced stories; recent events told from preliminary reports only, blaming no one beyond the report.
6. Respectful: no gore, no victim close-ups; end on what changed.
