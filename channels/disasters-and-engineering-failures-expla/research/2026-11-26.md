## What top long videos do that we don't (titles and thumbnails)
| | Title | Thumbnail |
|---|---|---|
| The breakouts above (15–45x) | The **event's searchable name** every time, plus one concrete stake ("25 People Died", "Final 10 Minutes", "270 Feet"), and often a promise of **3D** | Not opened tonight (YouTube refused the reads). Last Altitude's and Time of Impact's titles promise a 3D or inside-the-cockpit view |
| Ours | Hyatt and Titan name the event. "One Phone Call Doubled the Load" and "What Really Happened" carry no figure | A flat cartoon of the object, which is clean but generic. Our 3D kit's type-accurate shot isn't on the thumbnail |

- **Text on the thumbnail:** we already do what the thumbnail studies support. [Eden's 507K-video study](https://eden.so/blog/what-makes-a-good-youtube-thumbnail/) found that only 6.3% of breakouts repeat their title on the thumbnail, and that 62% of breakout thumbnail text is in all caps.
- **What we lack:** the searchable event name every time, a figure in the title, and the 3D picture on the thumbnail. Small reconstruction channels win on that combination, and a long video lives on search and browse, not the Shorts feed.
- **Proposal:** `dd-long-title-event-3d`, below.

## Doable changes, ranked
1. **`dd-long-title-event-3d`** (experiment, long videos, area title; S; judged on views7d and impressions CTR):
   - **Title:** name the event as people search it, plus one sourced stake, plus "in 3D" when the video has the reconstruction.
   - **Thumbnail:** cut it from the 3D moment-of-failure shot, with the failing part ringed in red and at most 3 words.
   - **Who acts:** for Studio's packaging step, and for the improver to implement.
2. Carried over (Shorts loop, `cycle/backlog.json`): `dd-stakes-first-hook`, `dd-hold-after-hook`, `dd-pause-before-reveal`, `dd-bed-voice-only`, `dd-title-figure-clock`, `dd-short-75s`. These still fit the promo Shorts.

## Not doable
- **Thumbnails built from news photos or other channels' frames:** licence rules (Commons only).
- **"Last words" titles quoting cockpit audio from an open investigation** (the Learjet trend): speculation, which the profile forbids.
- **"Most horrific accidents" compilations:** gore and horror framing, which breaks the limited-ads rule.
