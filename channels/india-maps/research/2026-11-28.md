## Policy and algorithm (tonight's topic)
- **Inauthentic content (in force since 16 July 2026):** the Partner Program now spells out "generic, repetitive or template-based videos with little meaningful variation between uploads", whether made with AI or templates. It isn't a ban on AI. For us the risk is the every-state fill, our most template-like format (3 of 7 Shorts so far). What protects us: `variety.py` already keeps a category out of the last 3 videos, every payoff is a different sourced fact, and every beat is directed. No change needed. I'll keep the every-state share in view once there are numbers.
- **Synthetic-content label:** YouTube asks for disclosure only for *realistic* AI content. "An animation … in a fully animated video" is "not realistic" and needs no label. Our flat animated maps with a generic Kokoro narrator (not a real person's voice) fit that, so `containsSyntheticMedia: false` in `workers/lib/youtube.ts` is right for this channel. India's IT Rules amendment (in force 20 Feb 2026) covers content that "appears real or indistinguishable from an actual person or real-world event", which our videos are not. A May 2026 draft would make labels visible for the whole video, but I found no sign it was finalised. Nothing to adopt.
- **Monetisation from 1 Feb 2027:** new applicants need 20M Shorts views in 90 days (or 8,000 hours), and every member needs 10M Shorts views in 90 days for a share of Shorts ad revenue. This is an `owner` item (notification, info).
- **Algorithm, from the market data:** the breakouts are small channels beating their subscribers 50–370×: TavoQuiz with 538 subs has 368K, Naksha Nama with 2.6K subs has 763K, Drowsy Atlas with 1.3K subs has 305K. Shorts are still shown on the video's own pull, not on the channel's size. That's good news for a new channel.
