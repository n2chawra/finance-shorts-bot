# Why the channel hasn't taken off, and what to change

## Context

You asked for a full review of the project after about a month: what is wrong, and what to add,
delete or improve. I studied the analytics, the scripts and frames of the videos, the live channel
page, the agents and experiments, the git history (about 1,080 commits) and outside reporting on
Shorts. Numbers are from GitHub `main` on 11 Nov (your local copy is 225 commits behind).

**Where it stands:** 14 subscribers, 50 Shorts with about 13,500 views, 4 long videos with 25 views.

## What is wrong, in order of importance

1. **No Short has ever passed YouTube's first test.** Five October Shorts stopped at 1,148–1,190
   views and nothing has gone higher. About two in three people shown a Short swipe away (engaged
   views are 37% of views), and the whole channel has 1 comment and 4 shares (count as of 5 Nov).
   The videos are not yet strong enough for the feed to widen them. This is the core problem;
   everything else makes it worse.
2. **Since 3 Nov most Shorts don't even get the first test.** Of 16 Shorts, 13 have 4–64 views,
   including two that held 66–67% viewed. Your Studio screenshots rule out a restriction and make
   the API upload an unlikely cause (see the Studio section below).
3. **Nothing ran long enough to learn from.** Shorts length changed 10 times in 22 days, the look
   about 7 times, the audience 3 times, the channel name 3 times. The recipe behind the only good
   week (21–25 Oct: 20–35 s, the cast acting a skit, big word captions, tempo 1.4, median 454
   views) was replaced after 5 days.
4. **The tests can't give real answers at this traffic.** Detecting a 1.5× lift needs about 150
   videos per side; decisions were made on 2 vs 1, 4 vs 24 and 7 vs 7. The variant system flipped
   its own title and hook defaults within 2–3 days, and its "budget" drop also removed the trap and
   which-one-are-you formats that had 721 and 793 views.
5. **The machine takes the effort and the quota the videos need.** About 90% of commits are
   infrastructure. Nine routines share one Pro plan: usage limits caused six days with no videos
   (24–25 Oct, 5–8 Nov). About 160 coded checks plus three reviews mean 27% of steps fail (QA
   rejects 58%), a video takes 25–36 steps, and two rule systems deadlocked on 5 Nov.
6. **Long videos return nothing.** Eight attempted, four published, 25 views, 0 subscribers, with
   two agents and two experiments built around them.
7. **The videos look and sound like a template.** Same three characters standing at the bottom of
   every frame, one mandatory hook sentence ("You think X? It doesn't. Here's why") in 8 of 9
   Shorts from 3–5 Nov, a different narrator voice and music genre each video, invented people
   ("Rohan earns $5,000") more often than real stories, weak props (a crate of apples for Apple).
   This is also the profile YouTube's inauthentic-content enforcement targets.
8. **Packaging faults.**
   - Captions sit under YouTube's own channel name and Subscribe button (seen on the Neom Short).
   - The banner says "a new story every 2 days" and "60 seconds".
   - The tag "india" goes on every global video.
   - An Indian cast and a Hindi-origin concept present US-dollar stories.
   - No pinned comment, playlists or replies.

What the data says does work: Shorts of 35 s or less (median 729 vs 41 for 36–50 s), real
businesses and how they make money (gym 920, casino 835, McDonald's 469, jeweller 1,148),
billionaire questions, and skits where the cast do the talking.

- **The October "hits" were failed tests, not hits.** YouTube showed them to about 1,100 people
  and three in four swiped. Growing Shorts are usually said to need well over half staying
  (unofficial rule of thumb: 60–70%+). None of yours is there.
- **After a run of failed tests YouTube now tests most uploads on tens of people, not a thousand.**
  That is the likeliest reading of November; I can't prove it. It is not the API upload (the gym
  Short got 920 through the API) and not a restriction. The flops' percentages rest on 10–30
  viewers, so they are noise.
- **YouTube doesn't know who the videos are for.** The search terms that led to them are "fifa
  mobile 26", "animal costumes", "the simpsons intro", "cooking". The Shorts are being tried on
  random viewers, who swipe.
- **So the fix is the first one to two seconds** (the first frame and first sentence decide the
  swipe) and a subject YouTube can recognise from the title and opening. Everything after second
  three matters less until this is fixed.

## Is it the animation? Side-by-side with channels that are growing (watched 11 Nov)

**No. Your animation is more elaborate than theirs. The gap is the idea, the title and the first
three seconds.**

| | MoneyTalk (518 subs, 75 videos) | The Curious Plot (14.5K subs, 36 videos) | Yours |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typical Short | 20K–80K views, best 701K | 2.4K–175K views | 4–64, best 1,190 |
| Picture | real photos over a looping background, one-word captions | very simple blob figures on a plain field | full cast, sets, props, camera |
| Title | the whole surprising fact with the name: "The NBA Paid Magic Johnson $40M. He Made A Billion" | a puzzle with numbers: "₹0 to ₹62,400 a Month Selling Lunch — He Never Cooked" | hides the subject: "The basketball star who got rich off the court" |
| First 3 s | the real face and the fact in one sentence | the contest set up: four brothers, zero rupees each | a false claim to knock down, then "Watch this." |
| Body | named specifics (cinemas where chains wouldn't go, Starbucks) | one question kept open: why did one make 6× more? | generic chips ("LIFE INSURANCE", "TEAM STAKES") |

Same story, same week: their Magic Johnson Short has 70K views, yours 419. Seen in yours:
- a grey placeholder head on Magic Johnson's card (it reads as a missing picture);
- a near-empty room at 8 s;
- a cheque half off the frame at 12 s;
- two text buttons as the whole picture at 18 s;
- captions cut off by YouTube's channel row in three of eight frames;
- an office worker and an uncle who have nothing to do with the story.

What both growing channels have that yours lacks:
- **One repeatable idea a viewer recognises.** MoneyTalk: a surprising true money fact about a
  famous name. Curious Plot: a parable where the same start gives different results.
- **Their look, voice and format have stayed the same.**
- Even MoneyTalk has duds at 23–193 views (about 1 in 10), so some untested uploads are normal;
  13 of 16 is not.
- MoneyTalk's 2.5M+ views brought only 518 subscribers: fact Shorts bring views, not followers.
  The Curious Plot's story series converts far better.

## Recommended changes

### A. Freeze one recipe for 14 days (the biggest change)
- One recipe, about 30 Shorts, no default changes in between: 25–35 s, the cast act the skit, big
  word captions, tempo 1.4, a real named business or person, one payoff line, a question to the
  viewer at the end.
- The title and the first sentence state the whole surprising fact with the name and the number
  (no "You think X?", no "Watch this", no hidden subject). The first frame shows the real subject,
  recognisable at a glance; the cast appear only when they are part of the story.
- Every beat shows a named specific thing, never a text chip standing in for one.
- Direction (you left it to me): one surprising true money fact about a real person or business
  per Short. Formats: how a real business makes money, billionaire questions, and traps or
  which-one-are-you (brought back) for at most one Short in three.
- One narrator voice and one music style for the whole channel.
- Turn the variant system and all A/B experiments off for the period. Review once, on day 14.
- New decision rule afterwards: change a default only on 20+ videos per side or a 3× difference.

### B. Two Shorts a day, the editor's best two
Fewer, better videos and room in the Pro quota. (Your 4 Nov choice was three; this reverses it.)

### C. Stop what isn't earning
- Pause long videos (Scout, Studio and their experiments) until a Short passes 10K views.
- Pause the dashboard agent and the monthly agent (it never ran).
- Research twice a week; improver only fixes your notes and bugs; auditor weekly.
- Shelve the multi-channel plan until this channel works.
- Don't use real photos of real people with a synthetic voice: likeness risk, and the upload would
  need the altered-content label that is hard-coded off today.

### D. Cut the gates to the ones that protect the channel
- Keep as failures: numbers, facts, layout, length, disclaimer, liveliness, uniqueness.
- Turn style checks in `generator/story.py` and `renderer/craft.py` into warnings.
- Remove the hook rotation rule that deadlocked with `improvement/arms.py`.
- Cut the writer's reading (about 100 KB) to one short contract with the conflicts removed
  (hedging, announcing structure, five different lengths, naming companies).
- Target: a video in 10 steps or fewer.

### E. Packaging fixes
- Move captions above the Shorts button zone (`remotion/src/fx/captions.tsx`,
  `remotion/src/design/frame.ts`) and check on a phone.
- Drop "india" from global videos and use one upload category (`workers/lib/publishing.ts`).
- New banner and description matching what the channel does now; keep the name as it is.
- Post a pinned question comment on each Short, and you reply to every comment.
- Figures as "$10 billion", not "$10,000,000,000" or "$500.0M".

### F. Steer by "stayed to watch"
- This is the one number that matters. Correction made while doing the work: the pipeline already has
  it. Engaged views ÷ views from the Analytics API matched your Studio screenshots within two points
  on all five Shorts I could compare (5% tax 44.5% vs 45.4%, gym 38.3% vs 38.1%), so no manual entry
  is needed; it is on the dashboard's Analytics and Impact pages.
- Target for the 14-day run: above 50% on most Shorts, above 60% on at least one. Views follow from
  that; judging by views alone is what misled the October decisions.
- Each Short's first two seconds are reviewed on their own before it ships (now check 6 of the QA
  review): would a stranger know the subject and want the answer with the sound off?

## Order of work once approved
1. Pull `main` (225 commits behind), keeping your uncommitted promo and remake files.
2. Settings and agent switches (B, C): through the dashboard settings, which sync to
   `config/settings.json`; `config/arms.json` is guard-locked, so you approve that edit.
3. Caption zone, tags, category, figure format (E).
4. Gates to warnings, the rule conflict, the slim writer contract (D).
5. The frozen recipe in `generator/prompts/`, `generator/story.py`, `generator/formats.py` (A);
   update `CLAUDE.md` and `PLAN.md` to match.
6. Banner art (`remotion/src/sheets/ChannelArt.tsx`); you upload it.

## How to check it worked
- `pytest` and the worker tests pass; `python -m generator.run state` shows the new settings.
- One Short made end to end in 10 steps or fewer; stills checked for captions clear of the buttons.
- Day 14: stayed-to-watch per Short from Studio (target above 50% on most, 60% on one), how many
  got a full first test (500+ views) and how many passed 1,200.

## Honest expectation
A month of templated, synthetic-voice finance Shorts at 14 subscribers is common, not unusual.
The changes above remove self-inflicted damage and give the October recipe a fair run; they don't
guarantee a breakout. From 1 Feb 2027 monetization needs 8,000 watch hours or 20M Shorts views in
90 days, so long videos will matter later, once Shorts bring an audience to send to them.
