## What top Shorts do that we don't
- **The first frame carries the hook.** The guides agree on one thing: the on-screen text in the first frame, the title and the description are what Shorts discovery reads, and the opening second decides the swipe. Our own rule says the same ("First frame hooks (big number or question)"). **v-20261021-04 breaks it:** at 0.0 s the frame shows two characters and the caption "POOR GUY GETS"; the chip "Raise: ₹20,000 a month" is in the script but appears later. Cause: the script writes `chip.at: "start"`, which isn't an event, so `at()` in `remotion/src/scenes/kit.tsx` falls back to 0.4 s, and `pop()` then springs it in. → `hook-text-on-frame-0` (adopt: it enforces an existing rule and fixes the fallback).
- **Titles:** the market's fastest Shorts this week are short and concrete: "Filing Bankruptcy on $1.3 Million of DEBT" (1.09M views in 4 days, 28 s), "India Apna Karz Chhap Kar Kyun Nahi Chukata? 🤯💸" (411K). Ours since the reset fit this (28–34 characters, a situation, one emoji). Our best older title by % viewed names a scene the viewer lived (a shopkeeper charging for UPI); the worst names an abstract claim. → `title-names-the-moment` (experiment, low confidence: confounded with voice and length).
- **Emojis:** vidIQ's 128.5M-video study: Shorts with an emoji averaged 38,993 views vs 26,125 without (+49%), long videos −11%. It's a correlation, and the article's own advice to skip emojis for finance channels isn't tested. Our planner already allows one in Shorts; no change proposed.
- **Hashtags:** sources disagree (3 in the title vs 3–5 at the start of the description; the first 3 in the description show above the title). Ours: 4–5 in the description, `#Shorts` first, which takes one of the three visible slots. Evidence too weak to propose anything; noted for a later look.
- **Length:** the references are 28–40 s; our reset Shorts are 27.5–28.2 s. Fine.
